# Binder: MTG Card Library Tool (Design Spec)

**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Status:** Approved in conversation; pending written-spec review

## 1. Purpose

Binder is a personal, single-user Magic: The Gathering collection manager that runs locally on a Mac or a Windows 10/11 PC, with an Android phone on the same Wi-Fi as its companion (M13). It answers four questions quickly:

1. **Do I own this card?** How many, which printings, and are they already in decks?
2. **What do I need to buy to finish this deck?** Account for cards committed to other built decks.
3. **What should I build next?** Brainstorm with Claude, grounded in what I actually own.
4. **How does this deck play?** Playtest it by hand against another of my decks, or alone.

Cards get into the library mainly by **scanning them with a camera over the scanning area**: an iPhone through Continuity Camera on the Mac, a USB webcam on the PC, or the Android phone's own camera (§5.10).

### Success criteria
- Scanning a stack is pleasant: capture about one card per 2 seconds, with identification running in the background. At least 90% of modern-frame cards are identified correctly, on the computer alone (measured on the Mac; on Windows, `pnpm ocr:bench` is still to be run, §5.1.6).
- Local library search returns in under 150 ms for typical queries.
- The deckbuilder's owned / in-another-deck / need-to-buy status and buy list are always correct under the allocation rules in §4.3.
- Brainstormed decks can be saved straight into the deckbuilder as prospective decks.

### Out of scope
Multi-user, remote access (anything beyond paired phones on the local network, §5.10), deployment, price history, trade or sale tracking, tracking card condition or language, offline card images (images load from Scryfall's CDN), a phone app of its own (a phone uses the computer's Binder, through the same pages made to fit it, §5.10, §5.11), rules enforcement or a computer opponent in playtests (§5.9).

M13 reversed two of the original non-goals for this fork (the owner's choices, 2026-10-02): network access, for paired phones on the local network only, never the internet; and a phone-friendly UI, the same pages made responsive rather than a separate app.

## 2. Decisions

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Platforms | **macOS**, and **Windows 10/11** (M13). The Mac keeps working as it did: every Mac-only piece is chosen per platform, not replaced, by `process.platform` in the server, the scripts and Electron, and by `src/web/lib/platform.ts` in the web app (the owner's choices, 2026-10-02). Linux runs the server and the tests (no scanning, no `pnpm app`). |
| Phone | On the Mac, an iPhone via macOS **Continuity Camera** (USB or wireless): the browser on the Mac sees it as a camera via `getUserMedia`. An **Android phone** is Binder's companion on either computer (M13, the owner's choice, 2026-10-02): paired with a code, it opens the computer's Binder over the Wi-Fi in Chrome (or as an installed web app), with every page usable by touch, and scans cards with its own camera into the computer's library (§5.10). It's not a standalone app. Nothing is installed on a phone. |
| Formats | Mixed. Each deck has a format, and the deckbuilder applies that format's rules. |
| Stack | TypeScript. **Vite + React** SPA, **Hono** server on **Node 24**, **SQLite** via `better-sqlite3`. |
| Card data | Local mirror of Scryfall **`default_cards`** bulk data (every printing, English or only-printed language; ~110k rows). Global search proxies the live Scryfall API. |
| Recognition | **On-device**: Apple Vision OCR via a Swift helper on the Mac; on Windows, Windows OCR (`Windows.Media.Ocr`) via a Windows PowerShell 5.1 helper, with no build step and nothing to install on Windows 10/11 with English (M13). Either is matched against the local mirror by the same matcher, unchanged for Windows until a Windows run of `pnpm ocr:bench` gives data. Scans it can't settle go to review. Scanning never calls Claude or any other service (the owner's choice, 2026-09-27: the Anthropic API is for deckbuilding help). |
| Claude model | `claude-opus-5-5` for brainstorm (the owner's choice, 2026-09-27), with server-side refusal fallbacks enabled (`fallbacks: "default"`). |
| Allocation granularity | By card identity (`oracle_id`), not by physical copy or printing. |
| Port | `127.0.0.1:4321` (localhost only), for the desktop app and `pnpm start` alike; it keeps every rule it had (§6 Requests). While phone access is on (§5.10), also `0.0.0.0:4322` for paired phones over HTTP, and `4323` for HTTPS when that's on; `BINDER_LAN_PORT` moves both (HTTPS on the next port up), and `BINDER_LAN=0` keeps phone access off whatever Settings says. |
| Desktop app | **Binder.app**, an Electron 44 app built on the Mac (`pnpm app`): its own window and a menu-bar icon, the server in Electron's utility process, the library in `~/Library/Application Support/Binder` (the owner's choices, 2026-09-28). On Windows (M13), the same app, packaged by electron-builder as a per-user NSIS installer that `pnpm app` builds and runs silently, with an icon in the notification area, and the library in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Binder`: Local, not Roaming (the library is large and belongs to that PC), and not Documents, which OneDrive syncs (the owner's choices, 2026-10-02). `pnpm start` still runs Binder from the terminal on the project's `data/`. |
| Phone access | Off by default; turned on in Settings → Phone access on the computer (or the tray). A phone pairs with an 8-digit code or a QR code, and may do everything a person does with their library, but not change the API key, backups, the library file, or phone access itself. HTTPS is optional, with Binder's own certificate authority limited to private addresses, which the owner installs on the phone once; without it everything but the live camera and installing Binder as an app works, and scanning takes photos. No firewall rule is added on Windows (it would need an administrator at every install): Windows asks the first time, and Settings explains the Private network requirement (the owner's choices, 2026-10-02). |
| Playtest | Played by hand: I pilot both seats (or one, to goldfish) and resolve every card myself. Binder enforces no rules, and there's no computer opponent and no outside game engine. Mulligans follow my playgroup's rule: draw 10, keep 7, as often as I like (the owner's choices, 2026-09-30). |

## 3. Architecture

```
iPhone ──USB (Continuity Camera)──► Mac camera ──┐
USB webcam ─────────────────────► PC camera ─────┴──► the desktop app's window, or a browser (React SPA)
                                                      │  HTTP + SSE, on 127.0.0.1:4321
                                                      │
                                                      │     Android phone: Chrome (the same SPA, and its camera)
                                                      │       │  Wi-Fi: HTTP on :4322, HTTPS on :4323 (§5.10)
                                                      ▼       ▼
                        Hono server (Node 24) ── SQLite  binder.db (§3.4: the desktop app's, or the project's data/)
                          ├── Scryfall API     (live search, bulk-data download)
                          ├── OCR helper       (a persistent child process: bin/ocr, Swift and Apple Vision, on the
                          │                     Mac; native/ocr.ps1, Windows PowerShell 5.1 and Windows OCR, on Windows)
                          └── Anthropic API    (brainstorm)
```

### 3.2 Commands
- `pnpm install`: builds nothing. better-sqlite3 loads the prebuilt binary it ships (it's in pnpm's `ignoredBuiltDependencies`), so no compiler is needed: no Visual Studio or Python on Windows. Only Electron and esbuild run their install scripts.
- `pnpm run setup` (not `pnpm setup`, which is pnpm's own command): get the OCR helper ready (on the Mac, compile `bin/ocr` with `swiftc`; on Windows, check that Windows OCR reads English, saying how to add it if not; elsewhere, one line saying this computer can't scan), run migrations, run the first Scryfall bulk import. A helper that isn't ready is said, and setup carries on, ending with a failing exit code as a failed import does.
- `pnpm dev`: Vite dev server plus the server (via `node --watch`), with the Vite proxy forwarding `/api` to the server (`changeOrigin: false`, `xfwd: true`: §6 Requests).
- `pnpm start`: build the SPA, then serve it and the API from the Hono server at `http://localhost:4321`, on the project's `data/` (saying so when the desktop app keeps its own library). Ctrl+C, and on Windows Ctrl+Break or closing the console window, stops it cleanly (§6 Startup).
- `pnpm app`: build the desktop app and install it (§3.4): Binder.app into `/Applications` on the Mac, refused while it runs; on Windows, its installer, run silently once a running Binder has quit. `pnpm app --no-install` leaves it in `release/` (`Binder.app`, or `win-unpacked\Binder.exe`). Elsewhere it says it builds only on a Mac or a Windows PC.
- `pnpm app:dev`: the desktop app's window run from the project, on `data/`, without packaging it. Like the app, it keeps the key in its library folder, `data/.env` (not the project's `.env`), and writes its window's files to `data/Electron/` and its log to `data/Logs/`.
- `pnpm move-library`: copy the library from `data/` to the desktop app's folder, once (§3.4).
- `pnpm icons`: draw the icons (`scripts/make-icons.ts`, Node with no dependencies): into `build/icons/`, the Windows `Binder.ico` and `tray.ico`, `icon.png`, and `tray.png`/`tray@2x.png`; and the web app's icons into `src/web/public/icons/` (rewritten only when their pixels change). On the Mac it first runs `scripts/make-icons.swift`, which draws `Binder.icns` and the menu-bar template.
- `pnpm test`: Vitest (§7).
- `pnpm ocr:bench`: OCR benchmark (§5.1.6).

### 3.3 Key dependencies
`hono`, `@hono/node-server`, `better-sqlite3`, `@anthropic-ai/sdk`, `zod`, `react`, `react-router`, `@tanstack/react-query`, `tailwindcss` v4, `vitest`, and for the desktop app `electron` and `electron-builder`. M13 adds `@peculiar/x509`, with the `reflect-metadata` polyfill it needs loaded before it, for phone access's certificates (§5.10), and `uqr` for the QR codes Settings → Phone access draws. Only the server's packages are runtime dependencies (they're what the desktop app ships); the SPA's, `uqr` among them, are built into it.

### 3.4 The desktop app (Binder.app, and Binder on Windows)
One Electron app on both platforms (M9; Windows in M13). What differs per platform is chosen in `electron/platform.ts`, a pure module tested for each platform on any computer; the Mac's app is as it was, but for the tray's phone items.

- **The app** (`electron/`): one window onto Binder's pages (at `http://localhost:4321`, as from the terminal), a tray icon (the Mac's menu bar; Windows' notification area), and Binder's server (`startBinder`, `src/server/start.ts`) in Electron's utility process, so the window and the icon stay responsive while the library does slow work. Closing the window keeps Binder running (scans finish, card data refreshes) and lets go of the camera; the Dock or the tray icon opens it again. Cmd+Q (Ctrl+Q on Windows) or Quit Binder stops the server first (it closes the library; 5 s at most), then the app; every quit waits until the server has exited, so a second one while it stops can't end it midway. One Binder at a time: opening it again brings its window forward. A launch with `--quit` (`Binder.exe --quit`, or the Mac app's binary with it), in any case, makes a running Binder quit as Quit Binder does; with none running it just exits. Web links (`http:`, `https:`) open in the browser and other links nowhere; Binder's own pages stay in the window.
- **Menus**: on the Mac, Binder, File (Close Window, Cmd+W), Edit, View (reload, zoom, full screen), and Window. On Windows (and Linux), where the Mac's app and Window menus mean nothing: File (Close window, Ctrl+W; Quit Binder, Ctrl+Q), Edit, View (as the Mac's; the developer tools only when run from the project), and Help (About Binder); on Windows the menu bar is hidden until Alt is pressed.
- **The tray icon's menu**, the same on both: Open Binder; **Phone access**, a checkbox that turns phone access on or off (§5.10; the server's process does it, as Settings would, and reports back); beneath it, while Binder listens for phones, a line with the address they open ("Phones: http://192.168.1.5:4322"), or why it can't listen, or why phones can't use HTTPS; **Phone access…**, which opens the window on Settings → Phone access (`/settings#phone-access`); and Quit Binder. The phone items wait for the server to be ready, and the checkbox can't be clicked while `BINDER_LAN=0` keeps phone access off. The tooltip names the phones' address while Binder listens for them ("Binder · phones: …"). On the Mac a click shows the menu, as menu-bar icons do; on Windows a click opens the window and a right-click shows the menu.
- **Windows' own**: the AppUserModelID (`local.binder.app`, the installer's shortcut's) is set before any window, so the window, its taskbar button and notifications are one app; the title bar is dark (`nativeTheme`; the Mac's is unchanged), and the window has Binder's icon. The first time the window is closed, a notification says Binder is still running and where its icon is (Windows 11 tucks a new tray icon under the arrow); `Electron/still-running-said` remembers it was said. When Windows ends the session (shutdown, restart, sign-out), a hidden window hears it, stops the server, and waits for it up to 3 s.
- **Starting**: until the server listens, the window says "Starting…", or "Backing up your library before upgrading it (a few seconds)…" while a migration's backup is saved (§6 Backups). A library that can't be opened, a port in use, or a `PORT` that isn't a port number is said in a dialog, in the server's one line, and Binder quits; for a port in use, the dialog says Binder may already be running from the terminal. On Windows, a port Windows refuses (one it keeps for Hyper-V, WSL or Docker) says so, with `netsh interface ipv4 show excludedportrange protocol=tcp` to list them, and that restarting the PC or setting `PORT` helps. Anything else that fails while the app starts is said in the dialog too, as is a server that stops unexpectedly. The window's process hands the server's process its paths in the `BINDER_APP_PATHS` environment variable (not on its command line), which it removes before starting the OCR helper.
- **Where things are**: the library is `appLibraryDir()`'s (`src/server/config.ts`, which every caller asks): on the Mac `~/Library/Application Support/Binder`, on Windows `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Binder` (elsewhere `~/.local/share/Binder`). It holds `binder.db`, `backups/`, `bulk/`, `scans/`, phone access's `lan/` (§5.10), and the API key's `.env`, with the window's own files in its `Electron/` and the server's log in `Logs/binder.log` (the run before in `binder.previous.log`; on Windows, a log another program holds open is copied and emptied instead of renamed, or appended to). Run from the project (`pnpm app:dev`), it's the project's `data/`. `BINDER_DATA_DIR` and `PORT` override both, for checks.
- **Camera and clipboard**: only Binder's own pages may use the camera and write to the clipboard (the Copy buttons); every other permission, the microphone included, is refused, and every camera request is answered (no, on any failure). On the Mac, the camera is the Mac's permission, asked for once (`NSCameraUsageDescription`). A camera refused (by mistake, or after an update) isn't asked for again: the Scan page says to turn Binder on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera, then quit and reopen Binder; if its switch is already on and the camera still won't start, `tccutil reset Camera local.binder.app` in Terminal, then reopening Binder, clears the old permission. The app declares Continuity Camera (`NSCameraUseContinuityCameraDeviceType`), which macOS requires before it lists an iPhone to an app. On Windows nothing is asked: Settings → Privacy & security → Camera has a switch for every desktop app, and Binder refuses the camera only when Windows reports it denied or restricted (the Scan page then names Camera access and Let desktop apps access your camera). Chromium's fake camera (the end-to-end check's) needs no permission.
- **Building**: `pnpm app` draws the icons (`scripts/make-icons.ts`: a 9-pocket binder page in stone and amber; on the Mac it runs `make-icons.swift` too, §3.2), builds the SPA with Vite's API, and packages with electron-builder's (the files as they are, no asar; the server's runtime packages only, with only that platform's better-sqlite3 build and none of its sources; each platform's OCR helper and icons). Each step that fails says so in one line.
  - On the Mac: it builds the OCR helper into the app (so the app never needs Xcode's tools), signs it ad hoc, and replaces `/Applications/Binder.app`, refusing while it runs.
  - On Windows: a per-user NSIS installer (`release\Binder-Setup-<version>.exe`, one click, no administrator prompt) that installs to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\binder`, with Start menu and desktop shortcuts and an entry in Settings → Apps; uninstalling never deletes the library (`deleteAppDataOnUninstall` stays false). `native/ocr.ps1` ships as a file, which Windows PowerShell can read. A running Binder (found with `tasklist`) is asked to quit (`Binder.exe --quit`, from the installed copy or the one just built) and given 10 s, both before packaging (electron-builder starts by deleting `release\win-unpacked`, which Windows refuses while a Binder runs from it) and before installing; one that doesn't quit is a refusal saying to quit it from the tray. The installer runs with `/S`; its exit code becomes one line, and `Binder.exe` must be there and newer than before, or it says to open the installer to see why. `package.json` names an author, so Windows doesn't list Electron's. Nothing is signed, so SmartScreen may warn on another PC. No updater files are written (`win.publish: null`).
- **Moving the library** (once): `pnpm move-library` copies `data/` to the desktop app's folder: the database through SQLite's backup (whole even while its log holds changes), checked, and `backups/`, `bulk/`, and `scans/`, all staged in the folder's `.moving/` and put in place (the database last) only once the whole copy has worked; a copy that fails changes nothing, and it can be run again. On Windows each removal and rename while putting it in place is retried while another program holds a file (§6), and one that still fails says to run it again. It refuses while Binder runs (the app's process, by `pgrep` or `tasklist`, or anything answering on 4321 or on `PORT`), when there's no library to move, or when the folder already holds a library with anything added to it (one the app made before the move, with nothing added, is replaced). `data/` is left as it was. Neither the key nor `lan/` is copied: the key is entered again in Settings, and phones pair again. On Windows, it also says when an earlier run copied the library to `%USERPROFILE%\Library\Application Support\Binder`, a folder nothing there opens.

## 4. Data model

SQLite runs in WAL mode with `foreign_keys=ON`. Migrations are numbered SQL files applied at startup and tracked in `schema_migrations`. An existing library is backed up before a migration upgrades it (§6).

### 4.1 Tables

**`cards`** is the Scryfall mirror, one row per printing.
| column | notes |
|---|---|
| `id` TEXT PK | Scryfall card id |
| `oracle_id` TEXT | card identity; indexed. For reversible cards without a top-level `oracle_id`, use the first face's |
| `name` TEXT | full name (`A // B` for multi-face) |
| `face_names` TEXT | face names separated by a newline, for OCR matching of the front face |
| `lang`, `layout`, `released_at` | |
| `set_code`, `set_name`, `collector_number` | index on (`set_code`, `collector_number`) |
| `rarity` | common, uncommon, rare, mythic, special, bonus |
| `mana_cost`, `cmc` REAL | multi-face: faces' costs joined with ` // ` |
| `type_line`, `oracle_text`, `flavor_text` | multi-face: faces joined with `\n//\n` |
| `power`, `toughness`, `loyalty` | TEXT (can be `*`, `1+*`), plus numeric shadow columns `power_num` etc. (NULL if non-numeric) |
| `colors`, `color_identity` | TEXT of WUBRG letters in canonical order (`''` = colorless) |
| `keywords` | JSON array |
| `legalities` | JSON object |
| `games`, `finishes` | JSON arrays |
| `artist` | |
| `prices` | JSON `{usd, usd_foil, usd_etched}` |
| `image_normal`, `image_small`, `image_art_crop` | front-face URIs (multi-face: first face) |
| `card_faces` | JSON (for rendering back faces) |
| `purchase_uris`, `scryfall_uri` | |
| `is_promo`, `is_digital` INTEGER | |

Also `card_names`, one row per card identity (`oracle_id`): `name`, `face_names`, `search_name` (the name lowercased, without punctuation or diacritics), and `default_card_id`, the printing shown by default (not a promo, not dated in the future, not a special product, English, newest). Its FTS5 index `card_names_fts` (`tokenize='trigram'`, external content over `card_names.search_name`) serves name autocomplete when the query has a word of 3 or more characters; shorter queries use `LIKE` prefix matching. Nothing indexes rules text: `o:` and name terms in the query language scan `cards` with `instr` over `oracle_text` and `search_name`. OCR candidates don't come from the index either: they come from the fuzzy name search over `card_names` (§5.1.2).

**`collection`** holds what I own.
| column | notes |
|---|---|
| `id` INTEGER PK | |
| `card_id` TEXT FK→cards.id | printing |
| `finish` TEXT | `nonfoil`, `foil`, or `etched` |
| `quantity` INTEGER | > 0 (rows at 0 are deleted) |
| `added_at`, `updated_at` | |
UNIQUE(`card_id`, `finish`).

**`decks`**: `id`, `name`, `format`, `status` (`prospective`/`built`), `notes`, `created_at`, `updated_at`.

**`deck_cards`**: `id`, `deck_id` FK (cascade), `oracle_id`, `preferred_card_id` (nullable FK→cards.id, used for the image and price display), `quantity`, `board` (`commander`/`main`/`side`/`maybe`), `category` (nullable free text). UNIQUE(`deck_id`, `oracle_id`, `board`).

**`scan_items`**: `id`, `image_path`, `status` (`queued`/`identifying`/`confident`/`review`/`committed`/`discarded`), `method` (`ocr`/`manual`; the schema also allows `claude`, unused since scanning is on-device only), `ocr_json`, `candidates` (JSON array of `{card_id, score}`), `card_id` (chosen), `finish`, `quantity` (default 1), `confidence` REAL, `error`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `reason` (why a `review` scan needs a look: `printing` or `unsure`), `auto` (1 for auto-mode captures), `deck_id` (nullable FK→decks; the deck it goes to besides the collection, set null when that deck is deleted) and `board` (`commander`/`main`/`side`), `auto_committed` (1 when auto-commit added it), `lifted` (1 for an auto-mode capture taken after auto mode saw the empty mat: the card before it was lifted). Migrations 003, 005, and 006 added the last six.

**`ai_threads`**: `id`, `title` (empty until the first message names it), `deck_id` (nullable; a deleted deck unlinks it), `created_at`, `updated_at`.
**`ai_messages`**: `id`, `thread_id` FK, `role`, `content` (JSON array of content blocks, stored verbatim including thinking blocks, except as §5.5 says: a declined answer is stored empty, as is the marker that a conversation has grown too long for Claude, a stopped one as its text and any fallback markers, a cut-off one as its text, one that fell back partway with only text before the fallback, and empty text blocks are dropped), `meta` (JSON, for an assistant message: the model that wrote it, why it stopped (the API's stop reason, or `stopped` when the owner stopped it), and its token usage; added by migration 004), `created_at`.

**`playtest_game`** (migration 007) holds the game in progress (§5.9), at most one: `id` INTEGER PK (always 1), `setup` (JSON: the model version, the seed, both seats' deck snapshots, starting life, the starting seat, and whether it draws on turn 1), `created_at`.
**`playtest_actions`** (migration 007): `game_id` FK→playtest_game (cascade), `seq` INTEGER (0, 1, 2, … in the order played), `action` (JSON), `created_at`. PK(`game_id`, `seq`).

**`tokens`** (migration 008, M12) holds Scryfall's tokens and emblems, one row per token identity: `oracle_id` TEXT PK, `name`, `layout` (`token`, `double_faced_token`, `emblem`, or `flip` for a Role), `type_line`, `oracle_text`, `power`, `toughness`, `colors`, `image_small`, `image_normal`, `card_faces` (JSON), all from its newest paper printing that isn't dated in the future, in English if there is one. They're never in `cards`, so search, the library, and decks never see them.
**`card_tokens`** (migration 008, M12): `oracle_id` (a card) and `token_oracle_id` (a token or emblem it makes), from Scryfall's `all_parts`. PK(`oracle_id`, `token_oracle_id`).

**`lan_devices`** (migration 009, M13) holds the phones paired with Binder (§5.10): `id` INTEGER PK AUTOINCREMENT (never reused, so a forgotten phone's cookie can't name a phone paired after it), `name`, `token_hash` (BLOB: the HMAC-SHA256 of the phone's token under the secret in the library folder's `lan/secret`; the token itself is never stored), `created_at`, `last_seen_at` and `last_ip` (written at most once a minute), `origin` (the address it paired at, `http://192.168.1.5:4322` or `https://…:4323`, which its cookie belongs to), and `user_agent`. The secret, the ids of phones forgotten one at a time (`lan/forgotten`), and the HTTPS certificates are kept in `lan/`, outside binder.db, so a backup or a moved database can neither check nor make a phone's token.

**`meta`**: key/value. Holds `bulk_updated_at`, `bulk_source_updated_at`, `card_data_version` (the mapping version that imported `cards`), `card_names_version` (the default-printing rule that built `card_names`), `last_backup_at`, settings (`scan_auto_commit`, `scan_default_finish`, `scan_accept_uncertain_printing`, `buylist_ignore_basics`), and phone access's (M13, §5.10): `lan_enabled` and `lan_https` (`'1'` or `'0'`), and `lan_address` (the address phones open, when one is chosen; none means the recommended one).

### 4.2 Bulk import
1. `GET https://api.scryfall.com/bulk-data/default-cards` and read `jsonl_download_uri` (gzipped JSONL, ~79 MB compressed as of 2026-09-26). If that field is missing, fail with a clear error that names the fields actually present. Stream the download to `data/bulk/default-cards.jsonl.gz`.
2. Parse via `zlib.createGunzip()` → `readline`, one card JSON per line. Skip `is_digital` cards and cards where `games` lacks `paper`. Insert rows into a fresh `cards_staging` table in batched transactions. Tokens, double-faced tokens, emblems, and Roles (a type line starting "Token": Scryfall prints a Role as a flip card with a Role at each end) go to staging for `tokens` instead. Each card's `all_parts` entries with component `token`, and its `combo_piece` entries typed Emblem, go to staging for `card_tokens`; the reminder cards Scryfall also marks `combo_piece` are not linked (M12; art series and oversized cards are still skipped).
3. In **one transaction** (WAL readers keep seeing the old snapshot until commit):
   - Upsert staging into `cards` with `INSERT … ON CONFLICT(id) DO UPDATE`. Never use `REPLACE` or table renames, which would disturb foreign keys from `collection` and `deck_cards`.
   - Delete `cards` rows absent from staging **unless** they're referenced by `collection.card_id`, `deck_cards.preferred_card_id`, or `scan_items.card_id`, or are printings of a card (`oracle_id`) that a deck line uses and that's missing from staging altogether. Printings that Scryfall removes but that I own, chose for a deck line, or have in the scan queue are kept, and so is every printing of a deck's card that's gone from the new data (a line on the default printing names only the card). A dropped printing of a deck's card that's still in the data is deleted, so its frozen price can't win the buy list.
   - Rebuild `card_names` (with each card's default printing) and its `card_names_fts` index, drop `cards_staging`, and update `meta`.
   - Replace `tokens` and `card_tokens` with their staging (M12). A link whose token isn't in the data is dropped. `card_data_version` 3 marks card data imported with tokens, so older card data refreshes at the next start (below).

Refresh happens automatically in the background at server start if `bulk_updated_at` is older than 7 days or `card_data_version` shows the card data was imported by an older Binder mapping, and on demand from Settings. When Scryfall's `updated_at` matches `bulk_source_updated_at` and the downloaded file is still there, a refresh re-imports that file instead of downloading it again. Progress is exposed via `GET /api/bulk/status`.

### 4.3 Ownership and allocation rules (in `src/shared/ownership.ts`)
For a card identity `o` (`oracle_id`):
- **owned(o)** = Σ `collection.quantity` over every printing and finish with `oracle_id = o`.
- **allocated(o)** = Σ `deck_cards.quantity` for `o` across **built** decks (all boards except `maybe`).
- **free(o)** = owned(o) − allocated(o). This can be negative if built decks over-claim; the UI shows it as a conflict.
- **wantedBy(o)** = the prospective decks containing `o` (boards except `maybe`).

For a deck `D` and a card `o`, let `n` = the copies of `o` in `D` summed across all boards except `maybe`. Ownership is computed per card identity at the deck level, and every line of `o` in `D` shows the same status.
- If `D` is **built**: `available = free(o) + n` (D's own allocation counts toward itself).
- If `D` is **prospective**: `available = free(o)`.
- **short** = max(0, n − max(0, available)).
- **Line status**:
  - `owned` if short = 0.
  - `in_other_deck` if short > 0 and owned(o) ≥ n (the copies exist but other built decks hold them).
  - `buy` if short > 0 and owned(o) < n.
  
  The line always shows "own X, Y in other built decks".
- **Maybe board**: lines show status computed as if prospective with `n` = that line's quantity. They're excluded from allocation, completion %, and the buy list.
- **Buy list** = all lines with short > 0: quantity = short, price = the cheapest `usd` across that card's paper printings, leaving out memorabilia (gold-bordered World Championship cards, Collectors' Edition, 30th Anniversary Edition) unless nothing else has a price (fall back to `usd_foil`, then `usd_etched`), plus a TCGplayer link from `purchase_uris`. Basic lands are excluded when `buylist_ignore_basics` is on (default on).
- **Mark as built**: compute short for every line. If any are > 0, show the list and require confirmation. Marking built never changes `collection`.

### 4.4 Format rules (in `src/shared/formats.ts`)
| format | deck size | max copies | sideboard | commander | legality key |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| commander | exactly 100 incl. commander(s) | 1 | none | 1–2 (partner/background) | `commander` |
| standard / pioneer / modern / legacy / vintage / pauper | min 60 | 4 (vintage: restricted = 1) | ≤ 15 | none | same name |
| casual | none | none | any | optional (not checked) | none |

Basic lands, and cards whose oracle text says "A deck can have any number of cards named", are exempt from copy limits. Commander color identity: every card's `color_identity` must be a subset of the union of the commanders' identities. Violations appear as warnings on lines and in a deck-health panel. They never block edits.

## 5. Features

### 5.1 Scanner (`/scan`)

**5.1.1 Capture (browser)**
- A camera picker lists `enumerateDevices()` video inputs, and the choice is remembered in `localStorage`. The iPhone appears as "<name> Camera" once Continuity Camera is active, named after the phone (so a renamed one needn't say "iPhone"). With no choice remembered, the page picks the best by label (`cameraRank`, M13 for Windows and phones): an iPhone, a phone's or tablet's back camera ("camera2 0, facing back" on Android, a Surface's "Rear"), or a document camera; then any other camera (a USB webcam, a renamed iPhone); then the computer's own, facing the owner (a Mac's FaceTime, MacBook, iMac or Studio Display camera; a Windows laptop's "Integrated Webcam", "HD User Facing", HP TrueVision or Wide Vision, Lenovo EasyCamera, Chicony, "USB2.0 HD UVC WebCam" or plain "HD WebCam"; a phone's front camera); Desk View and virtual cameras last. On a phone (Android, or a touch screen) with nothing remembered, the page first asks for the back camera (`facingMode: environment`) and uses that one, without remembering it. It shows a short help note when it finds only the computer's own cameras (§5.1.5). A camera plugged in or out updates the list without opening a camera.
- The live camera needs a secure page with `getUserMedia` (`CAN_USE_LIVE_CAMERA`): the computer's own address, or a phone over HTTPS (§5.10). A camera that won't start says what to do where the page runs: on the Mac, System Settings (the app) or the address bar's camera icon (a browser); on Windows, Settings → Privacy & security → Camera's Camera access and Let desktop apps access your camera (the app), or the address bar's camera icon and then that switch (a browser), and a camera "in use" there names the switch too; on Android, the icon left of the address → Permissions → Camera.
- A camera that stops (unplugged, or the iPhone moved away) says so and stays the chosen camera: the page doesn't switch to another one by itself, and Capture is off meanwhile. While that camera is still listed, **Start it again** (which a camera error offers too) restarts it; once it's gone from the list, the page says it starts again by itself when it's back (and, with other cameras listed, that another can be chosen above). Choosing another camera in the list uses that one, and a reload while the page waits opens the best-ranked camera present. A camera that stops sending pictures for a while says so until it picks up. A resized picture (an iPhone turned) keeps the guide in place.
- Live `<video>` preview with a card-shaped guide overlay (63:88 aspect ratio). Capture crops the guide region, plus an 8% margin on each side so a card placed a little off the guide stays whole, from the full-resolution frame, encodes JPEG at quality 0.92, and POSTs it to `/api/scan` (with `?auto=1` from auto mode, `lifted=1` when auto mode saw the empty mat since its last capture, and `deck` and `board` when scanning into a deck, §5.1.3).
- **Photos** (M13): without a live camera (a phone over plain HTTP, which has no `navigator.mediaDevices`), the page takes photos instead: **Take a photo of the card** (a file input with `capture=environment`, which opens the camera app) and **Choose a photo**. With a live camera, a phone offers **Take a photo** beside it. The photo opens upright (`createImageBitmap` with its EXIF orientation); one too large to open whole opens at half size, and says so, and one the browser can't read (HEIC) says to turn off HEIF photos in the camera's settings. A full-window review shows it with the same amber guide, to fit over the card: one finger drags the guide, two move and size it, and a Size slider, the mouse wheel, the arrow keys and +/− do too. **Use photo** crops the guide plus the live capture's 8% margin, shrinks it so the card is at most 2000 px tall (never enlarging), and sends a JPEG at quality 0.9 through the same `POST /api/scan` (the phone, not the computer, crops: the photo is the whole table at 12 to 50 megapixels, often on its side). The next photo held the same way (upright or sideways) starts with the guide where it was last fitted. Retake (Choose another), Cancel, Escape and Back close the review, and Cancel while the capture is being made sends nothing. The review is drawn outside the page, which it makes inert, as Layout's own overlays do.
- **Manual mode**: Space or a button captures. The `a` key switches between Auto and Manual (§5.7). On a touch screen the hint says to tap Capture.
- **Auto mode**: every 150 ms the page samples the guide region at low resolution, each dot the average of a block of pixels (so a one-pixel camera wobble over a card's text isn't movement), and compares samples with any overall brightness shift taken out (so the camera adjusting its exposure isn't either). The first picture that holds still for 600 ms is the empty mat (the line under the camera says "clear the guide to start" until then; choosing Auto again, or starting the camera again, learns it again). A picture that holds still for 600 ms and differs clearly from the empty mat is a card, and is captured, unless it's the card captured last, still lying there: the empty mat not seen since and the picture about the same. So a hand passing over a card doesn't capture it again, a card moved or swapped is captured, and the empty mat itself is never captured; a still picture close to the empty mat updates it, following slow changes in the light. A capture after the empty mat showed says the card before it was lifted (`lifted=1`). The line under the camera says what auto mode is doing: learning the empty mat, ready for a card, waiting for the card to hold still, or captured. A capture by hand (Capture or Space) in auto mode counts as its capture of the card showing. The thresholds were measured with an iPhone over a mat: a card lying still changes by at most about 6 from sample to sample, and differs from the empty mat by about 60. Captures give audio and visual feedback; a beep that can't play within a quarter second (before the page has been clicked, the browser holds sound back) is left out rather than played late.
- On a touch screen a remembered Auto isn't restored, and the button reads **Auto (mounted)**: auto mode is for a camera held still over the mat, not a phone in the hand.
- Below `lg` (a phone, a tablet) the capture button sits in a bar that stays above the bottom tabs while the camera is on screen, with the queue's counts ("2 ready · 1 to check · Add"), which scroll to the queue; the bar publishes its height so toasts stand above it. On a narrow screen a new capture's row is scrolled into view, and the live preview is at most half the screen tall.
- The queue list updates live by polling `/api/scan/items` (the whole queue, which stays small) every 1 s. Polling was chosen over SSE here because it's simpler and fast enough.

**5.1.2 Pipeline (server)**
`POST /api/scan` saves the image to `scans/<id>.jpg` in the library folder (the project's `data/`, or the desktop app's, §3.4), inserts a `scan_items` row with status `queued`, and returns immediately. A worker processes the queue (OCR concurrency 2). A phone's captures have limits of their own (§5.10).

1. **OCR**: send the image path to the persistent OCR helper (§5.1.4). The response lists text lines with normalized bounding boxes and, from Apple Vision, confidences.
2. **Match** (`src/server/scanner/matcher.ts`, pure and unit-tested):
   - Positions are measured over the card's text (from the top of the highest line to the bottom of the lowest), so a card that doesn't fill the capture still reads right.
   - **Collector line**: from lines in the bottom 20% of the card, parse the collector number ("145/269 R", "U 0201", or, on 2003–2014 cards, the "66/350" at the end of the copyright line; never the power/toughness box), the set code (a 3–5 character alphanumeric token next to `•`/`★` and a language code like `EN`), and the copyright year ("© 1993-2009" gives 2009). A `★` in place of `•` marks a foil. On-device OCR reads the `★` as `*`, so either one presets the finish to foil.
   - **Title**: lines in the top 12% of the card. Take the longest text, normalized (lowercase, strip punctuation, fold diacritics).
   - **Scoring**: candidates come from (a) an exact `(set_code, collector_number)` lookup (when it finds nothing, set codes one character away are tried, for OCR slips like TDM read as TOM) and (b) the exhaustive name search shared with decklist import (every name scoring ≥ 0.75), top 5. Each candidate is scored by name similarity (Jaro-Winkler against the name and each face name).
   - **Decision**:
     - `confident` if the set code and a collector number read name exactly one printing that passes the name check. When the title names a card (exactly, or with similarity ≥ 0.92 and a gap of ≥ 0.05 to the next *different* oracle card; a name several card identities share names all of them), the hit must be that card. Otherwise its name must be ≥ 0.80 similar to the title, or its full name (for a multi-face card, two of its face names) must be printed exactly as a line: split cards print their names sideways, and flavor-name cards print the real name small. Every collector-number candidate on the collector line is tried.
     - Otherwise the title must name one card: exactly (its full name or a face's), or with similarity ≥ 0.92 and a gap of ≥ 0.05 to the next *different* oracle card. A name several card identities share is low confidence, with all of them as candidates. Then the printings are narrowed: English ones only (OCR reads English titles; a card with none keeps them all but can't be `confident`), then by the set code, the collector number, and the copyright year (that year or the next) when read. A filter that would leave none contradicts the rest, so the printing stays uncertain. `confident` when one printing is left and nothing contradicted.
     - `review` (printing uncertain) when more are left; the scan takes the first of them in the default printing order (not a promo, not dated in the future, not a special product, English, newest). If the `scan_accept_uncertain_printing` setting is on (default off, useful when scanning a stack of old-frame cards whose printing I don't care about), these become `confident` with that printing instead. It never accepts a reading that contradicted the title (a filter that would have left none): that is the one sign the title may be misread, so the scan stays in `review`.
     - Anything else is **low confidence**: `review`, with the candidates attached (the likeliest first).
   - The thresholds are constants tuned with the benchmark (§5.1.6).
3. **OCR failure** (the helper missing, crashing, or timing out; or no helper for this computer at all, §5.1.4): `review`, with `error` set ("OCR failed: …"; a failure of the matcher itself says "Couldn't match the card: …"). The capture is never dropped, except an auto-mode capture in which OCR finds no line with at least three letters: the bare scanning area, which auto mode captures only when it took something else for the empty mat. It's kept discarded with its OCR result as the mark (§5.1.3), and the queue notes how many such captures it skipped in the last minute.
4. **A result that can't be saved at all** (the database failing, so not even for review) is logged and tried again: after 5 s, then twice as long each time, up to 5 minutes, until it's saved. The scan never stays identifying for good.

**5.1.3 Queue UI**
Each row shows the captured thumbnail next to the Scryfall image, the name, set and number, what one copy costs (the printing's price in the chosen finish, "each" for several copies, or "no price"), a confidence badge, a printing dropdown (all printings of that oracle card, newest first, with set name and number), a finish toggle (options limited to the printing's `finishes`, default from settings, preset to foil when the foil star was read: `★`, which on-device OCR reads as `*`), a quantity stepper, and a discard button.

- **Scanning into a deck.** Above the camera, "Scanning into" chooses where captures go besides the collection: the collection only, one of my decks and a board (Main, Sideboard, or Commander for a Commander or Casual deck), or **New deck…**, which creates a built deck (its cards are in hand). The choice is remembered for the rest of a scanning session (8 hours; a reload or a visit to another page keeps it), with the deck's creation time, so a new deck that reuses a deleted deck's id never inherits it, and forgotten once its deck is gone; a deck's **Scan cards into this deck** opens the page with that deck chosen. Each scan keeps the deck it was captured for, and its row says so ("For Elf Ball · Main", with **Change** to move that one scan), so switching decks never moves scans already taken. A capture for a deck deleted meanwhile goes to the collection only.
- A `review` row with a card offers **Looks right**, which accepts it as it is; like a card picked by hand, it then reads "Ready · confirmed". A "Check the printing" row also offers **Different card…**, and a "Not sure" row lists its candidates; both give a search box to pick any card by hand.
- A row whose OCR failed shows the error and **Try again**, which reads it again.
- **Send every scan here to…** (above the queue) moves every scan in the queue to one deck and board, or to the collection only, for a session begun with the wrong deck. It starts from where new captures go, and new captures then go where it sent the scans.
- An auto-mode capture of the same printing as the scan before it, itself an auto-mode capture (still in the queue or already added) taken within a minute, is marked: "Same card as the scan before it. Discard it if the camera caught one card twice." A hand reaching in, or a nudge to straighten the card, can make auto mode capture one card twice. Scans I discarded after they were read are skipped, so discarding a scan between two copies can mark the second; a capture auto mode took after seeing the empty mat (`lifted`, §5.1.1), a bare-mat capture between the two (dropped, §5.1.2), or a scan I discarded before it was read, means the card was lifted: the next copy of the same card, as when scanning a deck's four, isn't marked.
- **Commit** ("Add N cards to collection", or "Add N cards (M also to Elf Ball)") adds the ready rows the button counted: the `confident` rows, including `review` rows I've confirmed. A row that became ready after the count waits for the next commit. It upserts into `collection`, incrementing quantity, sets the rows to `committed`, and deletes their images (an image that can't be deleted is logged; the cards were added all the same). A scan with a deck also goes to that deck, in the same transaction: its copies first fill the copies of that card the deck lists on that board that earlier scans into it haven't (so scanning a deck planned first doesn't list its cards twice), and only the copies beyond those are added to the line; a new line shows the printing scanned. The toast says how many went to each deck.
- The `scan_auto_commit` setting (default off) commits a manual capture as soon as it's `confident`, to its deck too. Auto-mode captures always wait in the queue, so a double capture is never committed unseen. While the Scan page is open it says what auto-commit added (the queue lists what it added in the last minute) and refreshes the collection and decks.
- Discarding also deletes the image. On Windows, where antivirus or the indexer can hold a file a moment, deleting is retried (a commit's images together within 2 s, since it waits on them), and at start Binder removes images left behind by scans already committed or discarded (never one a row still waiting names, nor a file no row names).
- At narrow widths (the queue's own width under 28rem: a phone, or half a tablet) a row is a grid: smaller images, the badge on its own line, the controls across the row beneath. On a touch screen every control in a row is at least 40 px tall, and Discard is a bordered button away from the badge.

**5.1.4 OCR helpers (`native/ocr.swift`, `native/ocr.ps1`)**
A persistent process that reads one JSON request per line on stdin (`{"id": "...", "path": "..."}`) and writes one JSON response per line on stdout:
```json
{"id":"...","width":1234,"height":1720,"lines":[{"text":"Lightning Bolt","confidence":0.98,"box":{"x":0.06,"y":0.04,"w":0.5,"h":0.04}}]}
```
- **Which helper** (`ocrHelper()`, `src/server/scanner/ocr-helper.ts`; M13): on the Mac, `bin/ocr`, built from `native/ocr.swift` with `swiftc` when Binder runs from the project and it's missing or older than its source (`[scan] Built the OCR helper`); the desktop app ships it built. On Windows, `native/ocr.ps1`, run in Windows PowerShell 5.1 by its full path (`%SystemRoot%\System32\WindowsPowerShell\v1.0\powershell.exe -NoLogo -NoProfile -NonInteractive -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File …`; never `pwsh`, PowerShell 7, which can't reach WinRT), with no build step; the desktop app ships it as a file. Elsewhere there's none, and every scan fails with "Scanning reads cards with Apple Vision on a Mac or Windows OCR on a Windows PC; this computer has neither."
- **Apple Vision** (`ocr.swift`): `VNRecognizeTextRequest` with `recognitionLevel = .accurate`, `usesLanguageCorrection = false`, and `recognitionLanguages = ["en-US"]`. The image is decoded upright (its EXIF orientation applied, M13) and at most 4096 px on its long side, and the size reported is the upright one.
- **Windows OCR** (`ocr.ps1`): `Windows.Media.Ocr` through WinRT, with the en-US recognizer, else any English one. With none, the error says to add English (United States) in Settings → Time & language → Language & region, or only its text recognition (`Add-WindowsCapability -Online -Name 'Language.OCR~~~en-US~0.0.1.0'`, as administrator), and the next request tries again. The image is read into memory (so it can be deleted at once) and decoded upright; a small one is enlarged to about 2400 px on its long side (at most 2.5×), and none is larger than the engine takes (`OcrEngine.MaxImageDimension`). Vision reads the columns of a card's collector line as lines of their own, which the matcher expects, so each Windows OCR line's words are split where a gap is wider than 1.5× its tallest word, each part a line with its own box. Windows OCR measures no confidence: `confidence` is 1. Answers are written as ASCII bytes (`\uXXXX` escapes, numbers in the invariant culture whatever the PC's language, NaN and Infinity as 0). Each image has 8 s, past which its WinRT operation is cancelled. Under a device policy that runs PowerShell in Constrained Language mode, it answers once that the policy keeps Binder from Windows OCR, and exits, so each image says why. `-Check` says which languages Windows OCR reads here (`pnpm run setup` uses it), and `-SelfTest` checks the script's own parts in any PowerShell.
- Boxes are normalized with the origin at the top-left. `confidence` is optional.
- Errors produce `{"id":..., "error": "..."}`.
- The Node side (`ocr-client.ts`) keeps the process alive and sends it one image at a time, so each image's 10 s timeout starts when the helper starts on it: a slow image never times out the one behind it. The first image sent to a newly started helper gets 30 s instead (Windows PowerShell and WinRT starting cold). A crash or a timeout fails the image being read and restarts the process; the images waiting go to the new one. A helper that exits fails the image being read only once its output has been read, so a reason it gave (the device policy) is that image's error. An answer with no id (a request the helper couldn't read) is about the image being read, and a result the helper can't encode (a NaN) comes back as an error rather than not at all. Since M13 the helper runs with no console window (`windowsHide`); requests are sent as pure ASCII (`\uXXXX` for anything else, so a path with "Zoë" in it survives any code page); answers are read past a byte order mark, CRLF and blank lines, and each is checked against its shape (one that isn't fails its image with "The OCR helper answered something unexpected", and the helper keeps running); and the last 2,000 characters of anything else the helper printed, with its stderr, go into its errors.

The iPhone should be locked, stationary, and in landscape orientation to be offered as a camera. Mount it over the scanning area.

**5.1.6 Benchmark (`pnpm ocr:bench`)**
Downloads about 70 Scryfall `large` images across frame eras (the 1993, 1997, 2003, and 2015 frames, 2020 and later, showcase/borderless, and split/DFC/adventure), runs OCR and the matcher, and prints accuracy per era and per decision. It also lists every card read as foil. Scryfall's image of a printing that comes in nonfoil shows the nonfoil card, so only foil-only printings should be listed. Only the modern collector line prints the `★`; an older foil-only printing prints none, so it isn't read as foil either. Used to tune §5.1.2 thresholds. Real phone captures will be noisier; the thresholds get re-checked with my own scans.
- It reads with this computer's helper (§5.1.4), or with `--helper <file>`: any program that speaks the protocol, or a PowerShell script run as `native/ocr.ps1` is (a changed copy of it, to try other values). With no helper here it says so in one line.
- It reads one image first, untimed (a helper's cold start), then times the rest (median and slowest).
- Each card's reading is saved beside its image as `<set>-<number>.<engine>.json` (`apple-vision`, `windows-ocr`), a line of the file for each line of text, so two engines' readings can be compared with a diff.
- On the Mac, M5's run: 66 of 70 identified with their printing, 4 old cards with the right card and the printing to check, 0 wrong. Windows OCR hasn't been benchmarked yet: the matcher stays as it is for Windows until it has (`docs/plans/m13-followups.md`).

### 5.2 Search (`/search`; `/library` is the same page locked to My library with a stats header)

**5.2.1 Scopes**
- **All cards**: `GET /api/search/scryfall?q=&page=` proxies `GET https://api.scryfall.com/cards/search` with `unique=cards`. Results are annotated with ownership (owned, free, decks) by looking up `oracle_id` locally. Digital-only cards are left out of each page, so Scryfall's total counts some cards the pages don't show: beyond one page it reads "About N". A page past the last is empty, with a way back to the first. Library-only terms (`in:`, `free`, `qty`, `is:wanted`, `is:unpriced`) get a hint that Scryfall reads them its own way, with a button to search My library.
- **My library**: `GET /api/search/library?q=&view=cards|printings&sort=&page=` runs the local compiler over `collection ⋈ cards`. Filters evaluate per owned printing and finish row. The `cards` view groups by `oracle_id`, summing quantities; the `printings` view shows each owned row.
- **Offline fallback**: if Scryfall is unreachable, All cards offers "Search local card data instead", which runs the same compiler over `cards`, on what's typed in the search box, with library-only filters disallowed.

**5.2.2 Query language (Scryfall-compatible subset), in `src/shared/search/` (parser) and `src/server/search/compile.ts` (AST → parameterized SQL)**

| syntax | meaning |
|---|---|
| bare words, `"quoted phrase"` | name contains (each term ANDed) |
| `!"Exact Name"` | exact name |
| `o:` / `oracle:` | rules text contains; `~` stands in for the card's own name |
| `t:` / `type:` | type line contains |
| `c:` / `color:` with `: = >= <= > < !=` | colors. `:` means `>=` (including). Values: `wubrg` letters, color names, guild/shard/wedge names, `c`/`colorless`, `m`/`multicolor` |
| `id:` / `identity:` / `ci:` | color identity. **`:` means `<=`** (fits within), matching Scryfall |
| `m:` / `mana:` | mana cost contains these symbols (`m:{G}{G}`, `m:2WW`) |
| `mv` / `cmc`, `pow` / `power`, `tou` / `toughness`, `loy` / `loyalty` with `= != < <= > >=` or `:` | numeric comparisons (on numeric shadow columns) |
| `r:` / `rarity:` with comparisons | common < uncommon < rare < mythic |
| `s:` / `set:` / `e:` / `edition:` | set code |
| `f:` / `format:` / `legal:`, `banned:`, `restricted:` | legality |
| `a:` / `artist:`, `ft:` / `flavor:`, `kw:` / `keyword:` | artist, flavor text, keyword |
| `usd` with comparisons | price (nonfoil usd) |
| `is:` | `commander` (legendary creature, or text says it can be your commander), `permanent`, `spell`, `dfc`, `split`, `promo` |
| `a b`, `a or b`, `-a`, `( … )` | AND (implicit), OR, NOT, grouping. OR binds looser than AND |
| **Library only:** `in:deck`, `in:built`, `in:prospective`, `in:"Deck Name"` | card identity appears in any, built, prospective, or named deck |
| **Library only:** `free`, `qty` / `own` with comparisons | free(o), owned(o) |
| **Library only:** `is:foil`, `is:nonfoil`, `is:etched` | finish of the owned row |
| **Library only:** `is:wanted` | in a prospective deck with short > 0 |
| **Library only:** `is:unpriced` | the owned row's finish has no price: the copies the library value leaves out |

Unsupported keys produce an error that names the key. They are never silently ignored. The parser returns positioned errors for malformed input, which the UI underlines.

### 5.3 Collection management
- Manual add and remove via the detail drawer.
- The `/library` stats header shows total cards, unique cards, total value (Σ quantity × price for each finish), and the last-added date. When some copies have no price, it says how many, as a link that searches the library for them (`is:unpriced`, one row per printing and finish).
- **CSV import**: auto-detects headers, case-insensitive. Quantity from `Count`/`Quantity`/`Qty`. Name from `Name`/`Card Name`. Set from `Edition`/`Set`/`Set Code`/`Set Name`. Collector number from `Collector Number`/`Card Number`. Finish from `Foil` (truthy or `foil`/`etched`). A file with a set and a collector number needs no name column. Rows resolve by set+number, then set+name, then name (the card's default printing, §4.1). There's a preview step showing resolved, ambiguous, and unresolved rows before committing.
  - The file: Excel's `sep=` first line (padded with spaces or empty cells or not) sets the delimiter; a cell may have spaces before its opening quote; lines may end in CRLF, LF, or CR, inside quoted cells too. Its encoding (M13, read in the browser from the file's bytes): a byte order mark names UTF-8 or UTF-16 (little- or big-endian: Excel's "Unicode Text"); without one, UTF-8 unless it mostly isn't (more bytes UTF-8 can't read than letters beyond ASCII it can, so a stray byte doesn't garble the rest), and then Windows-1252, as Excel's "CSV (Comma delimited)" saves on a Western Windows PC.
  - TCGplayer: `Simple Name` is read before `Name` (a Name that isn't the Simple Name with a variant after it is noted). Trailing variant notes in parentheses or brackets ("Lightning Bolt (Borderless) [Foil]") are dropped one at a time, up to 8, then the rest at once, when the name as written matches no card (so a card whose own name ends in parentheses, like "Erase (Not the Urza's Legacy One) [Foil]", keeps them), and a misspelled name ending in parentheses is matched to the closest card whose name does (≥ 0.92, noted), not to the card named before its parentheses.
- **CSV export**: `Count,Name,Edition,Collector Number,Foil` (Moxfield-compatible), as `binder-collection-YYYY-MM-DD.csv` (local date). Text a spreadsheet could read as a formula (starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, or `@`, like "+2 Mace") is quoted. On Windows, Library also offers **Export for Excel** (M13), a link to the same export with `?excel=1`, which starts the file with a UTF-8 byte order mark: Excel on Windows then reads it as UTF-8 rather than in Windows' code page, so Lim-Dûl's Vault and Séance come out right. Without it, the file is as other apps' imports expect; Binder's own import takes either.

### 5.5 Brainstorm (`/brainstorm`, `/brainstorm/:threadId`)
- A conversation list plus a chat view. `POST /api/ai/threads/:id/messages` streams Claude's answer as server-sent events: text and note deltas, a line per tool call ("Searching your library for `t:elf id:g`"), and how each call went. Each tool round is stored as it completes (the call with its results, in one transaction), so a failure keeps everything before it.
- Model `claude-opus-5-5` with adaptive thinking at effort `medium` (Opus 5.5's default, which does better than Opus 5 at `high`), up to 64k output tokens, streaming, and server-side refusal fallbacks (`fallbacks: "default"`; the chat notes when another model carried on). After a fallback, Anthropic may keep answering that conversation with the other model for about an hour (a message says so only in its model, with no fallback block), so the chat also notes wherever the answering model changes. Thinking display `updates` returns Claude's short notes between tool calls, shown in the chat; its reasoning stays hidden. A replayed thinking block whose conversation no longer matches (the system prompt or tools changed in a Binder update) is dropped rather than refused (`block_binding.prefix_mismatch_behavior: "drop_block"`). The system prompt never changes between requests, so it stays cached with the tools. It explains the formats, the ownership rules, the tools and search syntax, and asks for card names as `[[Name]]`, brief answers, and a few words before each lookup.
- Binder runs the loop with the SDK's streaming Messages API: each request streams; then Binder validates and runs the tool calls, stores the round, and asks again, up to 16 requests per answer, Binder's own retries included (an answer that reaches the limit ends with an error, and Continue allows 16 more; the SDK's own retries after a failed attempt, §6, aren't counted, and a failed attempt isn't billed). (The SDK's tool runner can't store a round atomically, or drop what a declining model wrote before a fallback, without Binder taking over its history.) Tool inputs stream as Claude writes them; one that isn't JSON at all is asked for again, twice at most.
- **Tools** (Zod schemas, checked before running; a failed call goes back to Claude as an error result):
  - `search_my_library(query, limit?)`: the local compiler over the owner's collection, sorted by name (25 results unless Claude asks for up to 60, with the total). Compact rows: name, mana cost, type, power/toughness or loyalty, price, owned, free, and the decks using it, each with its id ("Elves (deck 3, built, 1)"). A query Binder can't parse comes back as an error to fix.
  - `search_scryfall(query, limit?)`: Scryfall's search, most-played first (EDHREC rank), with the same rows. Scryfall's warnings about ignored terms are passed on.
  - `get_card(name)`: the Oracle text (each face), mana value, color identity, the formats it's legal in, price, and the owner's copies and decks. Names resolve like decklist import (exact, face, fuzzy ≥ 0.92); an unknown name suggests close ones.
  - `get_deck(deck_id | name)`: one deck, by id or by name (ignoring case): format, status, notes, boards, completion, cost to finish, warnings, and every line with its board, category, and status. A name several decks share lists their ids; an unknown one lists the owner's decks.
  - `create_prospective_deck(name, format, notes?, cards[{name, quantity, board?, category?}])`: resolves names like decklist import. Names that match no card are left out and reported, and names matched only approximately (fuzzy) are reported with the card they became; with none matched, no deck is made. Creates a prospective deck with those lines and categories and returns its id, name, format, card count, completion, cost to finish (and how many cards to buy have no price), the deck's own format warnings, and up to 10 of its cards'. The chat shows the deck (and any names left out) with **Open in deckbuilder**; approximate matches reach the owner through Claude's answer.
  - Every tool checks its input with its schema before running. A problem comes back as a short, readable error (at most 5 problems listed), and the chat's activity line never fails, whatever Claude sent.
- **Deck-scoped conversations**: "Brainstorm with Claude" in the deck editor starts a conversation with `deck_id`, or reopens the deck's newest one that nothing has been said in yet and that I haven't named, so clicking it again doesn't pile up empty conversations (the start page does the same for conversations about no deck). The deck's summary (every line with its status) goes into a text block before the first message, not into the system prompt, so the cache holds; `get_deck` reads the deck again later.
- Messages are stored as the content blocks sent and received, and replayed verbatim, with these exceptions: an answer cut off (`max_tokens` or the context window) keeps only its text; before a fallback partway through an answer only text is kept (the API's rule for sending such an answer back); and a declined turn (everything after Claude's last finished answer, up to its refusal: the question, any tool rounds, and any earlier unanswered question) is left out of later requests, though the chat still shows it. A stopped answer keeps the text of the request in progress and where another model took over (that request's notes and any partly written tool call go; earlier tool rounds are already stored with theirs); a tool round already running when Stop comes finishes and is stored. An answer that fails partway, or is stopped before Claude wrote anything, can be continued: Continue asks again from what's stored. The reason an answer failed shows beside Continue in the window that saw it; after a reload the page says only that Claude hasn't finished answering.
- While Claude works with nothing new to show (before its first words, while it writes a tool call, after each tool round, after a notice), the chat shows "Thinking…". A tool call's line appears only once the call is written, which for a whole deck can take a while, so the stream says when Claude starts one.
- A conversation that outgrows Claude's context window says so ("This conversation has grown too long for Claude. Start a new one to go on."), whether an answer was cut off by it or the conversation can't be sent at all. Either way an assistant message (its text, or empty) records it, so the notice stays after a reload and Continue isn't offered: it would only send the whole conversation again. The message box gives way to **Start a new conversation** too, for the same reason (the server refuses a message there). An empty assistant message is never sent back to Claude.
- One answer at a time per conversation; **Stop**, or leaving the page, stops it. A Stop in the first instant, before the server has begun the answer, drops the page's stream instead, which stops it the same way. A window that opens the conversation while Claude answers in another shows "Claude is answering in another window…" until it finishes; a window already open on it isn't told, and a message sent from it is refused as busy. A conversation can't be deleted while it answers. `[[Card]]` links in answers open the card's drawer (`GET /api/cards/named`, which settles names that collide once punctuation is ignored the way the tools do: the whole written name first, then an exact name, then a card from a regular set). Each conversation shows an estimated cost, from each answer's token usage at the prices of the model that wrote it (each attempt of a refusal fallback at its own model's; an attempt declined before it wrote anything isn't billed, so it isn't counted), updated when an answer ends. It is usually a little under what Anthropic bills (Binder prices a model it doesn't know as Opus 5, and a refusal fallback's cache repricing isn't modelled): a stopped request counts little more than its input (or nothing, if stopped before Claude wrote any text), and a request that came through garbled or failed partway isn't counted.
- With no key, the page explains and links to Settings; stored conversations stay readable. On a paired phone (M13) it says to add the key in Settings on the PC, with no link, since only the PC sets it (§5.10).
- On a phone (M13): the conversations and the open one are shown one at a time below `lg`, behind a "← Conversations" link; on a touch screen Enter makes a new line and **Send** sends; a stream cut off while the page was hidden (the phone asleep) shows no error toast. The answers a phone asked for stop, as Stop stops them, when the PC forgets that phone (or every phone).

### 5.8 Sets (`/sets`, `/sets/:code`)
How complete the collection is, set by set (the owner's design, 2026-09-29).

- **Completion**: a set is complete when the collection holds a copy of every card in it: every card identity (`oracle_id`) among the set's printings in the card data, in any of its printings in that set and in any finish. So a showcase or borderless copy counts for its card, the basic lands count once each however many printings of them the set has, and a copy of the same card from another set (a reprint) doesn't count. A set's total is its number of card identities (Duskmourn: 276, of 417 printings). Copies in decks count: this is what's owned, not what's free (§4.3). Totals follow the card data, so a new set appears after a card-data refresh (§4.2).
- **Counted when asked**: `GET /api/sets` (the sets with an owned copy) and `GET /api/sets/:code` (a set and its cards) count from `cards` and `collection` on each request. No totals are stored, and there's no migration.
- **Percentages** round down, so only a complete set shows 100% (275 / 276 is 99%), and a set with cards owned but under 1% shows "<1%" (1 / 1,746 of Secret Lair).
- **Sets page** (`/sets`, **Sets** in the header after Search): one row per set with at least one owned copy, whatever its type (promo, commander, and Secret Lair sets too). Each row has the name, code, type, release month (the set's earliest printing), a progress bar, owned / total, and the percentage; a complete set's bar is gold, with a check mark.
  - Sorts: completion (highest first, the default, by the exact fraction), release date (newest first), name, or cards owned (most first). Ties go to the newer set.
  - A filter box matches a set's name or code ("No sets match" when none do). The sort and filter stay in the address, so coming back from a set keeps them.
  - With nothing owned, it says sets appear once cards are scanned or imported, with links to Scan and Library.
- **Set page** (`/sets/:code`: any set in the card data, owned or not; the code matches in any case): **← Sets**, the set's name, code, type, and release date, then the same bar and numbers.
  - **All | Missing only**, kept in the address.
  - One row per card, at its lowest collector number in the set, in number order (2 before 10, 7 before 7a). A row shows that printing's number, name, mana cost, and rarity, and the copies owned across the card's printings in the set, all finishes ("✓ 2"). A missing card is dimmed and says "missing".
  - A row opens the card detail drawer (§5.2.5) on that printing. Adding or removing a copy there updates the page.
  - The whole set is one list, without pages (The List's 5,258 cards too). With Missing only on a complete set, it says the set is complete. A code that's no set shows "No set with that code" (404).
- **Later**: set symbols (they need Scryfall's set list), a card-image grid, and filtering by set type.

### 5.9 Playtest (`/playtest`)
A table for playing my decks by hand (the owner's design, 2026-09-30). I pilot both seats, or one seat to goldfish, and resolve every card myself: Binder moves cards, keeps counts, and remembers the game, but enforces no rules. Built for Commander first; 60-card decks play the same way.

**5.9.1 The game**
- **A list of actions.** A game is its setup plus the actions played, in order: draw, move a card, tap, add a counter, next turn, and so on. `apply(state, action)` in `src/shared/playtest/` turns them into the board. It's pure (no React, no database), so the page and the server run the same code. Replaying a game's actions always gives the same board as playing them one by one.
- **Shuffles** use a random number generator seeded when the game starts (the seed is in the setup; its state is part of the board). So a replay shuffles exactly as the game did, and undo or a reload never reshuffles a library.
- **Deck snapshots.** Starting a game copies both decks into its setup, so editing or deleting a deck mid-game changes nothing in it. Each copy of each card becomes its own game card with its own id, carrying the line's chosen printing (else the card's default printing, §4.1): name, faces (name, mana cost, type line, rules text, power/toughness, loyalty, small and normal images), whether it's a land, a creature, or another permanent type (from the front face), whether it's a commander, and (M12) the tokens and emblems it makes, from the card data (§5.9.8). Commander-board cards start in the command zone; the main board is shuffled into the library; side and maybe boards are left out. A line whose card is no longer in the card data (§5.4.2) is left out, and setup says how many were.
- **Stored in the library**: one game in progress at most (`playtest_game`, `playtest_actions`, §4.1). Routes:
  - `POST /api/playtest` starts a game (the seats' deck ids, the starting seat or random, starting life, whether the starting seat draws on turn 1), replacing any game in progress. The server picks the seed and, for random, the starting seat.
  - `GET /api/playtest` returns the game in progress (its setup and actions), or 404 when there's none.
  - `POST /api/playtest/actions` saves one action with its position (`seq`). A position other than the next one is refused (409). The server replays the game with the action added and refuses one that doesn't apply (400), so a stored game always replays.
  - `DELETE /api/playtest/actions/last?seq=` removes the last action (undo); a `seq` that isn't the last is refused (409).
  - `DELETE /api/playtest` ends the game.
  - `GET /api/playtest/tokens?q=` (M12) returns every token and emblem whose name holds the query, as game cards (§5.9.8): none for a blank query, at most 50. A query past 100 characters is refused (400).
- **The page applies each action at once** and sends it to the server behind any not yet saved, in order (§5.9.7).

**5.9.2 Setup and mulligans**
- **New game** (`/playtest` with no game in progress, or after End game): a deck for each seat (seat 2 may be **Nobody**, to goldfish), who goes first (**Random**, the default, seat 1, or seat 2), starting life (40 when either deck is a Commander deck, else 20; changeable), and **Starting player draws on turn 1** (off by default). A deck needs at least 10 main-board cards. **Start game** replaces a game in progress after a confirm. The deck editor's **Playtest** opens it with that deck in seat 1.
- **Mulligans** (my playgroup's rule): the starting seat draws 10 and picks 3 to put on the bottom. Clicking a card marks it (1, 2, 3), clicking again unmarks it, and the first card marked ends up at the very bottom. **Keep 7** is on once exactly 3 are marked. **Mulligan** shuffles the 10 back and draws 10 again, as often as I like, and says how many mulligans so far. Then the other seat (if there is one) does the same, and turn 1 begins. Commanders stay in the command zone throughout.

**5.9.3 The board** (a mirrored table)
- The seat I'm viewing sits at the bottom of the window and the other seat at the top, mirrored. The board fills the window below the header without scrolling, and cards scale with the window.
- Each half has its **battlefield** and, at its right, a **side block**: the deck's name, life (with − and +), poison, commander damage taken from each enemy commander (with − and +), the tax on each of its own commanders, and its piles: library (card back and count), graveyard and exile (top card and count), and command zone.
- My **hand** runs face up along the bottom. The other seat's hand shows card backs and a count along the top.
- The **turn bar** between the halves: "Turn 6 · Krenko Goblins", then Undo, Log, Switch side, End game, and **Next turn**. The stack shows in the bar while something is on it (§5.9.5). Where the pointer is a finger (M13), it also has **Select** and **Clear** (§5.9.4), and below `lg` it puts Log, Switch side, and End game behind a ⋯ (a mouse keeps them at every width); the deck's name gives way first.
- **Screen size** (M13): where the pointer is a finger and the window is narrower than a tablet's (768 px), or the screen is under 500 px tall (by `device-height`, so a phone either way up, but not a tablet whose keyboard shortens the window), the board gives way to "Playtest needs a bigger screen: a tablet or the PC.", with **Show the table anyway**. Setup and the mulligans work on a phone (the mulligan three cards to a row, Keep and Mulligan in view). A narrow window on the computer still gets the table. While the board shows, the page doesn't pull to refresh.
- **Free placement**: a card on the battlefield stays where I drag it. Positions are fractions of its controller's half, measured from that seat's own edge, so resizing keeps the layout and the top half is drawn flipped. A card entering the battlefield goes to a default spot, beside the last card of its kind: lands in the row along its seat's edge, creatures in the row farthest from it, and other permanents between. Cards may overlap; the last one moved is on top.
- **Attached cards** tuck under their host, offset toward the far side, and move with it. When the host leaves the battlefield, they stay where they were, unattached.
- **Card faces**: a card shows its image, turned sideways when tapped, with its counters as small tags. A double-faced card shows its current face. A card whose image doesn't load shows a text frame (name, mana cost, type line, power/toughness). Hovering over a card I can see shows a large preview (the normal image) on the side of the board away from the pointer, with its counters listed (a mouse's only: a finger has View card, §5.9.4).

**5.9.4 Controls**
- **Drag** a card between hands, battlefields, piles, and the stack. Dropping on a library puts it on top. Dropping a permanent on the other seat's battlefield gives that seat control of it. A card always goes to its owner's hand, library, graveyard, exile, or command zone, whoever controls it.
- **Double-click** a card in the hand or command zone (not an emblem, §5.9.8) to play it: permanents go to their default spot on the battlefield, instants and sorceries onto the stack.
- **Click** a card on the battlefield to tap or untap it.
- **Select several**: drag a box over empty battlefield, or Shift-click. Clicking one taps them all (or untaps them all, when all are tapped); dragging moves them together, keeping their places; the menu acts on them all. Esc clears the selection.
- **Right-click a card** on the battlefield: tap or untap, flip (a double-faced card), turn face down or up, counters (+1/+1, −1/−1, loyalty, or a counter of any name, such as Midnight Clock's hour counters; add, remove, or set a number; the menu adds or removes one of each kind already on the card), attach to… (then click the host) or detach, copy (a token copy), create token… (§5.9.8), put an ability on the stack, move to (hand, top or bottom of library, graveyard, exile, command zone), reveal. In the hand: play, discard, exile, top or bottom of library, reveal.
- **The library** (right-click): draw N, look at the top N, search, mill N, reveal the top card, shuffle. Clicking it draws one.
  - **Look at the top N**: the cards in a dialog; each goes to the top (in an order I drag), the bottom, the graveyard, the hand, or exile. This covers scry and surveil.
  - **Search**: the library's cards by name (not in order); chosen cards go to the hand, battlefield, top, graveyard, or exile, then **Shuffle?** (yes by default).
- **Graveyard and exile**: clicking one opens its cards in a list to drag or right-click from.
- **Keys**, on the card under the pointer or the selection: `t` taps or untaps, `f` flips, `+` and `-` add or remove a +1/+1 counter. Anywhere on the page: `d` draws a card for the seat I'm viewing, Tab switches side, and Cmd+Z on a Mac, Ctrl+Z elsewhere (M13), undoes (the one shortcut with a modifier; Undo's tooltip names the one that applies). None of them while a text field or dialog has focus. Listed under `?` (§5.7).
- **Create token…** and the side block's buttons act for the seat whose half they're on.
- **By touch** (M13; a finger or a pen):
  - A press becomes a drag once it moves 10 px (a mouse's, 5), and the board keeps the press for itself (no scrolling or zooming the page); a second finger is ignored, and a press the browser takes back (`pointercancel`) ends with nothing dropped or tapped.
  - **Press and hold** (450 ms, about Android's own long-press) on a card, a library, a stack item, or empty battlefield opens what a right-click would. It takes the rest of the press: lifting doesn't tap or drop the card, and the browser's own long-press menu and the press's click are spent.
  - **A tap** on a card in the hand or command zone plays it (an emblem's opens its menu), and a tap within 500 ms of a play plays nothing, so a double-tap plays one card; a mouse still double-clicks. A tap on a stack item opens its menu with Resolve first, and on a card in a graveyard or exile list, its menu. A tap on the library draws; its ⋯ opens its menu. A finger swipes the stack strip sideways to reach the newest items.
  - **Select** (in the turn bar) makes taps on the battlefield add a card to the selection or take it out, and boxes add to it; **Clear** empties it. Attaching has **Cancel**, for every pointer.
  - A finger's card and stack menus have **View card**, the large preview that hovering gives a mouse, until a tap.
  - Where the main pointer is a finger, menus are sheets along the bottom, controls are 36 to 48 px, and a dialog focuses itself rather than a text field (a number's field still takes focus). Back closes a menu, a dialog, or the card viewed (one history entry for the three; the pile and log panels aren't Back's). The pile and log panels fit a narrow screen; a card in a pile's list scrolls it with a vertical swipe and drags out sideways. **Look at the top N** has ↑, ↓ and To top beside dragging, for every pointer.
- **Shift-click** (or Select) on a card already selected takes that card out of the selection (M13; it took the first card out). A Mac's Control-click on a card opens its menu without tapping it, and Control-click on a menu's backdrop is a right-click only on the Mac.

**5.9.5 Turns, the stack, and Commander**
- **Turn 1** begins once every seat has kept. The starting seat draws then only if the setup says so.
- **Next turn** moves the turn counter on and passes the turn to the other seat (goldfishing, the same seat). It untaps every permanent that seat controls, draws its card, and turns the board to that seat. There's no phase tracker. Goldfishing, Switch side is off.
- **The stack**: spells and ability markers (a small tag naming the card), newest on top. **Resolve** (a double-click, or the item's menu) sends a spell to its controller's battlefield (a permanent) or its owner's graveyard (an instant or sorcery), and removes a marker. A spell on the stack can also be dragged anywhere.
- **Commanders** start in the command zone. Each time one leaves the command zone for the stack or the battlefield, its tax goes up by 2; undo takes that back. Partners are counted apart.
- When a commander would go to the graveyard, exile, the hand, or the library, Binder asks **Command zone instead?** (**Command zone**, the default, or the place I chose). The action saved is where it went.
- **Commander damage** is entered by hand, per enemy commander, in the side block of the seat it hit.
- **Tokens and copies** that leave the battlefield or the stack vanish; an emblem stays in the command zone (§5.9.8).
- **Would lose**: a seat at 0 life or less, 10 poison or more, 21 or more damage from one commander, or that drew from an empty library gets a red marker. Nothing ends the game.
- **End game**: **Rematch** (the same decks, snapshotted again, with a new seed and the same settings; not offered when a deck has been deleted), **New game** (setup), or Cancel. Either one ends the game in progress.

**5.9.6 Hidden information and the log**
- Libraries are hidden, except to their own seat while it looks at the top N or searches.
- A face-down card shows its back to the other seat, and its face (dimmed, marked face down) to its controller.
- Switching side changes only the view: it isn't an action, so it isn't logged or undone. Coming back to `/playtest` shows the game from the active seat (from the choosing seat during mulligans).
- **Log** (from the turn bar): the actions in words, by turn, newest at the bottom. A line names a card only when the seat I'm viewing could see it ("Meren drew a card"; "Krenko cast Goblin Chieftain"). Reveal names the card to both seats.

**5.9.7 Undo, saving, and errors**
- **Undo** (Cmd+Z on a Mac, Ctrl+Z elsewhere, or the turn bar) takes back the last action, as far back as the game's start, mulligans included. Undoing an action not yet saved drops it from the queue.
- **Saving**: the page sends actions one at a time, in order. While the server can't be reached, a banner says "Couldn't save — retrying", the page tries again (1 s, then twice as long each time, up to 30 s), and later actions wait in line; the board keeps working.
- **A refused position** (409: the game changed in another window or on another device, a phone playing the same saved game, or a new game started there) reloads the saved game, with a toast saying why: "This game changed in another window or on another device, so Binder reloaded the saved game." (the server's own words: "The game changed in another window or on another device"). A game ended elsewhere (404) says it was ended in another window or on another device. An action the server finds doesn't apply (400) reloads it too.
- **An old game**: the setup records the game model's version. A game saved by an older model that the new one can't replay says so and offers only End game.

**5.9.8 Tokens**
- **Create token…** (a card's menu, or right-clicking empty battlefield), for that battlefield's seat: a form for name, power/toughness (optional), colors, type line, and how many. The token shows as a dashed text frame, without an image. **Copy** makes a token copy of a card, with its image.
- **Scryfall's tokens** (M12): the menu of a card on the battlefield (not face down) or a spell on the stack lists the tokens and emblems it makes (from `card_tokens`, carried in the deck snapshot) with their images, each one click to create one for the card's controller. Scryfall's Copy stand-in, a blank token many cards list, is left out: **Copy** makes the copy. **Create token…** gains a search over every token by name (the whole name first, then names that start with it), with the form kept for anything missing. An emblem goes to its seat's command zone, whose cards overlap when they don't fit the side block side by side; its menu puts an ability on the stack or removes it, and it isn't played. Until card data has been imported with tokens (§4.2), the lists are empty and the form still works.

### 5.10 Phone access (`src/server/lan/`, Settings → Phone access, `/pair`)
A phone on the same Wi-Fi uses the computer's Binder (the owner's design, 2026-10-02, M13): one library, one server, one scan queue, one game in progress; the phone is a browser. It's off by default, and while it's off nothing changes: Binder listens only on `127.0.0.1:4321`. Android in Chrome is the phone it's made for.

**5.10.1 Listening**
- **On and off**: Settings → Phone access (`PUT /api/lan {enabled}`) or the desktop app's tray (§3.4) turns it on, and `lan_enabled` (§4.1) keeps it on after a restart: startBinder starts it once the loopback listener is up. Changes are made one at a time, in order, so turning it off while it's still starting leaves it off. Nothing about it ever stops Binder: a failure is logged and shown in Settings.
- **The phones' listener**: `0.0.0.0:4322`, serving the same app, its requests marked as the phones' (`listener: 'lan'`, which the guard reads, §6 Requests). `BINDER_LAN_PORT` moves it (and HTTPS, on the next port up); one that isn't a port from 1 to 65535 is said instead of listened on. `BINDER_LAN=0` keeps phone access off for that Binder whatever Settings says: Settings and the tray say so ("Phone access is off for this Binder: it was started with BINDER_LAN=0"), and turning it on is refused (409 `lan_off`). A port it can't take says why: "Couldn't listen on port 4322: another program is using it. Set BINDER_LAN_PORT to use another port", or that Binder isn't allowed to use it (on Windows, it may be kept for Hyper-V, WSL or Docker).
- **Who may connect**: a connection is closed as it opens, before a request is read, unless it comes from this computer or from inside one of its private networks: a non-internal private IPv4 interface's subnet, no wider than its private block (a VPN's /0 counts as its 10/8). A connection from a public address is closed, whatever the router forwards.
- **The computer's addresses** (`lanAddresses()`): private IPv4 addresses (10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16; not link-local) on adapters that aren't internal, ranked Wi-Fi first (the phone is on it), then Ethernet, then other adapters, then a Hyper-V switch someone made, then virtual ones (Hyper-V's and WSL's vEthernet and the Default Switch, VirtualBox, VMware, Docker, Linux bridges, VPNs and overlay networks such as Tailscale and ZeroTier, Bluetooth). The first on a real adapter is recommended; virtual ones are listed, never recommended. They're read again every 30 s while Binder listens (Node has no event for a change), and when a request names an address Binder doesn't know or a connection comes from outside the known networks (at most every 2 s). The address phones open is the one chosen in Settings (`lan_address`) while it's the computer's, else the recommended one, else the first. When it changes, the log says so: a phone's cookie belongs to the address it paired at (§5.10.3), so phones paired at the old one pair again. A DHCP reservation in the router keeps it.
- **Windows' network profile** (`network-profile.ts`): for the adapter of the address phones open, `Get-NetConnectionProfile` in Windows PowerShell (`powershell.exe -NoLogo -NoProfile -NonInteractive -Command`, with UTF-8 output), read in the background on each poll and each status (never waited for: the first read takes a second or two), cached for a minute, and only for that adapter (the first profile Windows lists is often WSL's). A Public profile, whose firewall blocks phones, is `LanStatus.network.publicProfile`, which Settings warns about. Null elsewhere and on any failure. No firewall rule is added (§2): Windows asks the first time Binder listens.
- **The log** (`[phone]` lines, in the terminal and the desktop app's log): "Phones on this Wi-Fi can open http://192.168.1.5:4322 (pair them in Settings → Phone access)", or that it listens but the computer isn't on a network a phone can reach; "Paired "Pixel 8" from 192.168.1.23"; "Wrong pairing code from 192.168.1.40 (2 of 5)", and when an address or the code is done with; "Forgot "Pixel 8"", "Forgot every phone: each must pair again", a phone that forgot itself; "Phone access is off"; an address change; and HTTPS's (§5.10.5).

**5.10.2 Pairing**
- **A pairing window** (`pairing.ts`, in memory only: a restart closes it): `POST /api/lan/pairing` (PC) opens one, closing any other, once Binder listens (409 `not_listening`, with the listen error or "Turn on phone access first") and has an address (409 `no_address`). It holds an 8-digit code (`crypto.randomInt`) to type on the phone and a 128-bit key for the QR code's link, `http://<address>:4322/pair#k=<key>` (`https://…:4323` while HTTPS is on). The key is after the `#`, so opening the link doesn't send it: no request line, log or Referer holds it, and the pairing page posts it and removes it from the address at once. A window lasts 5 minutes and pairs one phone. An address with 5 wrong codes may try no more at it, and 20 wrong codes from anywhere close it (with 8 digits, strangers guess right once in 5 million windows), so one device on the Wi-Fi can't keep the owner from pairing; tries are also limited to 10 per 10 minutes from one address (429). Codes and keys are compared as SHA-256 digests in constant time; spaces and dashes in a code are ignored. `DELETE /api/lan/pairing` closes it.
- **Pairing** (`POST /api/lan/pair {code | key, name}`, on the phones' listener only; from the PC it's 404, as an unknown route): the name is 1–60 characters. 201 `{id, name}` with the cookie. A wrong code is 400 `wrong_code`, "That code isn't right: check the code on the PC and type it again (3 tries left)", or that too many wrong codes stopped pairing; no window is 409 `not_pairing`, saying whether none is open, it expired, or too many wrong codes closed it. A phone that pairs again replaces its old device.
- **The PC's dialog** (Settings → Phone access → Pair a phone): the QR code (uqr's matrix at error correction M, drawn as one SVG path with a 4-module quiet zone, whole pixels per module, about 200 px), the code grouped "4821 0937", a countdown, how the window ended (`pairingEnded`: "Paired “Pixel 8”.", the code expired, or too many wrong codes), Start again, and Done. It asks for the status every 2 s while it's open. Cancel, Escape and Back close it and close the window while the code still works, and so does the page going away (a `keepalive` DELETE); a click outside doesn't, as the phone may be typing the code. Settings shows the dialog again when a window is still open as it loads.
- **The pairing page** (`/pair`, `PairPage`): drawn outside the app's frame, where nothing polls. "On the PC: Settings → Phone access → Pair a phone"; the code field (numeric keyboard, `autocomplete="one-time-code"`, anything but digits dropped, shown grouped); the phone's name (its old one when it pairs again; else its model from `navigator.userAgentData` over HTTPS; else iPhone, iPad, Windows PC, Mac, or "Android phone"); the server's own words for a wrong code or no window; after a 429, the wait counted down on the Pair button; a failure to reach Binder says to check the Wi-Fi and that Binder runs. Over plain HTTP, a note says the live camera needs HTTPS and scanning works by photo. Opened from the QR link it pairs at once; a phone already paired is asked first: Pair again as …, or Go on to the library (the code isn't used up meanwhile). On the PC, `/pair` goes to Settings → Phone access.

**5.10.3 Devices and the cookie**
- **The token**: 32 random bytes; the cookie is `<id>.<token in base64url>`; `lan_devices` (§4.1) stores only HMAC-SHA256(secret, token). The secret is 32 bytes, in `<library>/lan/secret` (hex), private to the owner (mode 600, or an icacls access list on Windows, §5.6; the folder 0700), made when the first phone pairs. So a copy of binder.db (a backup, or one moved with the library) can neither check nor make a token.
- **The cookie**: `binder_device` over HTTP, `__Host-binder_device` with Secure over HTTPS (a browser keeps that one only when it's Secure, for the whole site and this host alone). HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict (a link or form from another site carries none), Path=/, no Domain, kept 400 days (the most Chrome keeps one), and sent again with a new 400 days once a day while the phone uses Binder. It belongs to the origin the phone paired at: another address, another port, or the other of HTTP and HTTPS is another site, with no cookie, and the phone pairs again there.
- **Forgetting**: the PC forgets one phone (`DELETE /api/lan/devices/:id`), or a phone forgets itself (`POST /api/lan/forget`, which clears its cookie too): its cookie stops working at once, and its id goes into `lan/forgotten`, so restoring an older backup can't let it in again (ids are never reused). Forget all (`POST /api/lan/forget-all`) makes a new secret first, so no cookie from before works. Each is written before the rows go, so one that can't be written leaves the phones as they were. Rows that can never let a phone in are dropped when first read: all of them when there's no secret (a library moved or restored without its `lan/`), and the ids forgotten. A phone forgotten while Claude answers it has its answers stopped (§5.5); its next request is 401 `unpaired`. Rename is `PATCH /api/lan/devices/:id {name}`.
- **Last seen**: the time and address of a device's last request, written at most once a minute. Devices are kept in memory between changes, as every request a phone makes checks its cookie.

**5.10.4 What a phone may do** (the route policy, `guard.ts`)
- Every API route has a policy: `public`, anyone on the network (`GET /api/health`, `GET /api/lan/me`, `POST /api/lan/pair`), limited to 120 a minute from one address; `device`, a paired phone: everything a person does with their library (cards, search, sets, the collection with its import and export, decks with theirs, the playtest, scanning, brainstorm, the scanner and deckbuilder settings, whether the PC has a key, a card data refresh, and forgetting itself); or `pc`. A route that isn't listed is `pc`, and a test fails until every route is listed.
- **The PC's alone**: the API key (setting and testing it: a phone mustn't swap in another account's), backups (their folder names the PC's account), the library file and compacting it (compacting holds Binder up while it runs), and phone access itself (a phone mustn't let in more phones, see the others, or let itself back in). A phone gets 403 `pc_only`, "Change this on the PC running Binder"; its Settings doesn't show those sections (§5.6), and `GET /api/settings/ai` gives it no hint of the key.
- **Limits**: a request without a working cookie is 401 `unpaired`, and after 60 a minute from one address, 429 `rate_limited` (with Retry-After). A phone's request body is at most 1 MB, 12 MB for collection and deck imports and their previews (413 `too_large`), and a capture's is the scan route's own 15 MB, whose answer says to take the photo again on a phone. A phone sends at most 2 captures a second (429), and none once 500 scans are waiting or their photos take 2 GB (409 `queue_full`, "The scan queue is full; review or add the scans on the PC first"), so a phone stuck capturing can't fill the PC's disk. It refreshes card data at most hourly: not when it's under an hour old, nor within an hour of a refresh a phone started (409 `recently_refreshed`).

**5.10.5 HTTPS**
Chrome gives the live camera, installing a site as an app, and the asynchronous clipboard only to a secure page. HTTPS is optional (`lan_https`, Settings → Phone access → Use HTTPS): without it everything else works, and Scan takes photos (§5.1.1).
- **The listener**: a second phones' listener on the next port up (4323), made with `https.createServer`, while HTTPS is on and the HTTP one listens; turning HTTPS off stops it alone. If it can't listen ("Couldn't listen on port 4323 for HTTPS: …") or its certificate can't be made ("Couldn't make the certificate for HTTPS: …"), phones use HTTP meanwhile, and `LanStatus.error` and a `[phone]` line say why, with `listening` still true. `POST /api/lan/https/rotate` starts it when it hadn't for want of a certificate.
- **Binder's certificate authority** (`certs.ts`, `@peculiar/x509` on Node's WebCrypto): ECDSA P-256, valid 10 years from the day before it's made, named "Binder on <computer> (YYYY-MM-DD)" (O=Binder; the date tells a rotated one from the one before it in the phone's list). It's a CA with path length 0 (it signs server certificates, never another authority), key usage keyCertSign and cRLSign, extended key usage serverAuth alone (so it can't sign mail a phone trusts), and critical name constraints permitting only: IPv4 10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16 and 127/8; the DNS names `local` (so `<name>.local`) and the computer's own names, leaving out a single-label name that could be a top-level domain on the internet (letters only, or `xn--`: a PC named `gaming` would otherwise let it vouch for all of `.gaming`); and email addresses and URIs only at `invalid`, which no real one is. Without those limits an authority trusted by the phone would let whoever held its key intercept the phone's traffic to any site. An authority on disk without them (or with a kind of name left unconstrained), damaged, mismatched with its key, or within 30 days of its end is replaced; one not valid yet (a PC clock set back) is kept; and files that are there but can't be read (another program holding them) fail the call rather than replace anything.
- **The server certificate**: ECDSA P-256, "Binder on <computer>", serverAuth, its names every address phones may open (a URL with an address sends no name to choose a certificate by), 127.0.0.1, and the computer's names, leaving out any the authority can't vouch for (a public or CGNAT address, IPv6, a renamed PC's new bare name), since one name outside its constraints would make phones refuse the whole certificate. 397 days from the day before it's issued, capped at the authority's end. It's issued again when the addresses or names change (on each address change), fewer than 30 days remain, the authority changed, or its files are damaged; it's checked at least daily, and a new one is served at once (`setSecureContext`), which phones don't notice.
- **The files**: `ca.pem`, `ca-key.pem`, `leaf.pem` and `leaf-key.pem` in `<library>/lan/`, each private to the owner and written to a temporary file that's renamed into place (retried on Windows, without blocking the server), one call at a time per folder; leftovers of an interrupted write are removed. Backups and the library move never carry them (§3.4, §6).
- **The phones' HTTP port while HTTPS listens**: pages are sent to the same host, path and query over HTTPS (302); the API and anything but a GET are refused with 403 `use_https`, "Open Binder at https://192.168.1.5:4323". Whatever HTTPS is doing, both phone ports serve two things of their own, to any phone, Host-checked only: `GET /phone-setup`, plain HTML from the server in Binder's colours (what the certificate is for and what it can't vouch for; Android's steps, Samsung's path, the screen lock, the "Network may be monitored" notice, Firefox; the authority's name and SHA-256 fingerprint in four lines of 8 bytes, to compare under Trusted credentials → User; Download binder-ca.crt; and Continue to Binder at `https://<same host>:4323/pair`, with its own words when HTTPS is off, has no certificate, or isn't listening); and `GET /binder-ca.crt`, the authority as DER (`application/x-x509-ca-cert`, an attachment named `binder-ca.crt`, not cached), 404 while HTTPS is off.
- **Pairing over HTTPS**: links are https://, and the cookie is `__Host-binder_device`. A phone paired over HTTP pairs again over HTTPS, and one paired over HTTPS pairs again when HTTPS is turned off (`LanDevice.https` says which way each paired). Pairing over HTTPS forgets the phone's HTTP device as well, when its browser sends that cookie too.
- **A new authority** (`POST /api/lan/https/rotate`, PC; 409 `https_off` while HTTPS is off, 500 `certificate_failed`): made and served at once; each phone removes the old one and installs the new one. `LanStatus.caReplaced` says this Binder replaced the authority (rotated, or remade because the old one's files were missing, damaged or near their end), and Settings says to install it again on each phone. `caFingerprint` ("AB:CD:…", SHA-256 of the DER, upper case) and `caName` name the authority in `lan/` while HTTPS is on, whether or not phone access is.
- **The log**: "HTTPS is on: phones open https://…. Each installs Binder's certificate once, from http://…/phone-setup; phones paired over HTTP must pair again", "Each phone installs Binder's certificate for HTTPS once, from …" (as it starts), "HTTPS is off: …", "Binder made a new certificate for HTTPS: …" (rotated, or remade), and "Couldn't renew the certificate for HTTPS: …".
- Nothing in Binder verifies these certificates: Electron's own TLS (BoringSSL's older verifier) refuses IP address name constraints, so a client inside the desktop app's server couldn't. Chrome on the phone verifies them with its own.

**5.10.6 Settings → Phone access** (on the computer only)
- **The switch**, with the server's refusal beside it. While it's on: whether Binder listens, or its error; the address phones open, as selectable text with Copy and a QR code (not a link: the desktop app's window mustn't leave Binder); a picker when the computer has more than one address ("192.168.1.5 (Wi-Fi, recommended)"), where choosing the recommended one stores no choice, so Binder follows it to another network, and a note when the one chosen before is gone; and the DHCP note. Firewall notes: on Windows, the Public-network warning, with Windows 11's and Windows 10's paths to make it Private (Wi-Fi's, or Ethernet's), and until a phone has paired, the tip that Windows asks the first time (allow Private networks; if cancelled, Windows Security → Firewall & network protection → Allow an app through firewall); on a Mac, until a phone has paired, that macOS may ask about incoming connections, again after a rebuild.
- **Pair a phone** (§5.10.2), and **Paired phones**, listed even while phone access is off (so a lost phone can still be forgotten): each renamed in place, with when it paired and was last seen ("Last seen 2 min ago from 192.168.1.23"), Forget with a confirm; and Forget all phones, with a confirm saying each must pair again.
- **HTTPS**, while phone access is on: the switch ("Use HTTPS (the live camera, installing Binder as an app, and Copy buttons need it)"). While it's on: the setup address with a QR code and Copy, the fingerprint, the notice that Binder has a new certificate, the Android steps (folded away), and Make a new certificate, with a confirm.
- **The status** (`GET /api/lan`, and the answer to `PUT /api/lan {enabled?, https?, address?}`; `LanStatus` in `src/shared/types.ts`): whether it's on and listening, `https`, the ports (`httpsPort` the real one while HTTPS listens; both 0 with `BINDER_LAN=0`), `error`, the addresses and the one chosen, `url` (what phones open; null unless Binder listens), `setupUrl`, `caFingerprint`, `caName` and `caReplaced`, the open `pairing` and how the last one ended (`pairingEnded`), the `devices` (each with `https`, how it paired), and Windows' `network`. Settings asks for it every 10 s, even while phone access is off (the tray can turn it on with Settings open), and every 2 s while pairing. The terminal and the tray get a `LanSummary` instead (`enabled`, `available`, `listening`, the phones' `urls`, `error`). An address that isn't the computer's is 400 `bad_address`.
- The tray's Phone access… opens `/settings#phone-access`, which scrolls to the section once its status has loaded, then drops the hash from the address.

**5.10.7 The app on a phone** (`ClientGate`, `src/web/lib/client.ts`)
- **Who's asking**, before the app draws: `GET /api/lan/me` answers `{client: 'pc'}`, `{client: 'device', id, name}`, or `{client: 'unpaired', https}`. The PC and a paired phone get the app, which knows which it's on (`useOnPhone()`); a phone that isn't paired gets only the pairing page. When the question fails, a page at this computer's own address (127.0.0.1, localhost, [::1]) shows the app as before phones (so the PC is never locked out, even by a Binder from before phone access); a phone's page says "Connecting to Binder on the PC…" and asks again (after 1, 2, 4… s, at most 10, or what a 429 says) until Binder answers, since guessing would show a paired phone the PC's parts or tell an unpaired one it was paired.
- **Losing the pairing**: a 401 `unpaired` from any query, change, or Brainstorm's stream (the PC forgot the phone, or every phone) brings the pairing page back, saying "This phone needs to pair with Binder again" and offering the name it had, and lets go of everything the app had fetched. Forgetting itself (Settings → This phone) brings the pairing page without that line. A 403 `pc_only` is said once in a toast, at most once in 10 s.

## 6. Error handling
- **Scryfall client**: every request sends `User-Agent: Binder/0.1 (personal)` and `Accept: application/json`. A single-flight queue enforces ≥ 100 ms between requests. On 429, back off exponentially (1 s, 2 s, 4 s), and after 3 retries give up and pause the queue for 8 s. The bulk download gives up when no data has come for 60 s (30 minutes at most in all). Network errors are marked `offline` for UI messaging. On Windows (M13), the server, `pnpm run setup` and `pnpm ocr:bench` trust the certificates Windows trusts as well as Node's own (`trustSystemCertificates`, with Node 24.5's `tls.setDefaultCACertificates`; best effort, and nothing changes with an older Node), so antivirus that checks HTTPS, or a company network, doesn't make Scryfall and Anthropic look offline.
- **Bulk import**: staging table plus atomic swap (§4.2). On failure the old data stays live and `meta.bulk_error` is set and shown in Settings. Offline with card data already here, the error says so ("… The card data from DATE stays in use"), and the start logs why the data is stale ("Card data is 9 days old") before refreshing in the background.
- **Startup**: a port already in use, or a `PORT` that isn't a port number, prints one line saying so and exits with status 1 (the desktop app says it in a dialog and quits, §3.4). On Windows, a port Windows refuses (EACCES) adds that it may keep it for Hyper-V, WSL or Docker, and that `netsh interface ipv4 show excludedportrange protocol=tcp` lists the ports it keeps. A library that can't be opened is said the same way, the library left as it was (`openDb` closes the file when a pragma or migration fails). Scans a restart interrupted are picked up again only once the server is listening, so a second Binder started while one runs leaves the running one's scans alone; images of scans already committed or discarded that a failed delete left behind are removed then too (§5.1.3). Phone access left on starts after that (§5.10.1).
  - **Where the library is** (M13, Windows only): before opening it, Binder warns, in a `[library]` or `[api key]` line, when the library folder is in OneDrive (which copies the database while Binder writes it and can hold its files open; it suggests `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Binder`), when the key file is (OneDrive would upload the key), when the folder is on a network share (`\\server\share`, where SQLite can't share its log safely), and when its path is long enough (210 characters or more) that the library's longest files would pass Windows' 260-character limit. It names `BINDER_DATA_DIR` to move it. It starts all the same.
  - **Stopping** (M13): Ctrl+C (SIGINT) and SIGTERM, and on Windows Ctrl+Break (SIGBREAK) and closing the console window (SIGHUP, after which Windows ends the process within seconds), stop Binder cleanly: both listeners close, then the OCR helper and the library; it logs "Binder stopped" and exits with status 0. A second signal, or a stop longer than 3 s, exits at once with status 1. A stop asked for twice waits for the one under way.
- **Requests** (M13: the guard, `src/server/lan/guard.ts`, replaces the loopback-only check; the code cites this section). There are two listeners, and a request is this computer's only when it came in on this computer's listener (`127.0.0.1:4321`), from a loopback address (`127.0.0.0/8`, `::1`, or IPv4 inside IPv6), and, if it has an `X-Forwarded-For`, its last hop is loopback too. Vite's dev proxy adds that header (`xfwd: true`), so a phone opening `vite --host` is a phone; anything else is a phone's request.
  - **This computer's requests** keep the rules they had: the Host header must name this computer (`localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `[::1]`: defeats DNS rebinding), and a request that changes something must come from Binder's own page (defeats CSRF): an `Origin`, when sent, must name the same host and port as the request, and `Sec-Fetch-Site`, when sent, must be `same-origin` or `none`; else 403 `forbidden`, "Requests must come from this computer". The dev server's proxy keeps the page's own Host (`changeOrigin: false`), so the rule holds through `pnpm dev` too. The web app's files are anyone's on it.
  - **A phone's requests** (the phones' listener, or a phone through the dev server's proxy): the Host must be an IP address, `localhost`, or this computer's name (`<name>` or `<name>.local`), else 403 `wrong_host` ("Open Binder at <the address phones open>"): this covers the web app's files too. The phones' listener then answers its own pages (`/phone-setup`, `/binder-ca.crt`) and sends phones on to HTTPS while it listens (403 `use_https` for the API, §5.10.5); the web app's files are otherwise anyone's there, as they're code, not the library. An API request that changes something must name Binder's own page as its `Origin` (required: over plain HTTP a phone's browser sends no other sign), with `Sec-Fetch-Site` `same-origin` or `none` when sent; a read with `Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site` is refused too (403 `cross_site`, "Requests must come from Binder's own page"). Then the route's policy (§5.10.4): `public` routes are rate-limited by address (429 `rate_limited`, with Retry-After); any other needs a paired phone's cookie (401 `unpaired`, which says to pair, or that phone access is off; 429 after 60 a minute from one address), counted through the dev server's proxy only while Binder listens for phones; and `pc` routes refuse a phone (403 `pc_only`). A phone's request bodies are limited in size (413 `too_large`). The guard sets who's asking (`pc`, `device`, or `unpaired`) for the routes, and notes when a phone was last seen.
  - Every response, refused ones included, carries `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'`, and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, and a `Cache-Control` when the route set none: `no-store` for the API, `public, max-age=31536000, immutable` for the built files in `/assets/` (a missing one is a 404, not the app's page, which that rule would keep for good), and `no-cache` for the rest (the app's page, the manifest, the icons). A path naming a Windows device (`CON`, `NUL`, `COM1`, `LPT1`, `CONIN$` and the like, in any segment, with any extension, case, trailing dots or spaces, or percent-encoding) or holding a `:` in a segment is never looked for on disk: it gets the app's page, so `/sets/con` (Conflux) still opens.
  - Ids in URLs are plain numbers from 1; `0x1`, `1e0`, and `01` are no id (404).
- **API key file**: the key replaces the first `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` line in place (keeping an `export`), removing any other; the rest of the file stays, with its own line endings (CRLF stays CRLF). A symlinked `.env` is written through to its target, a hard-linked one in place (made private before the key goes in); otherwise a temporary file is renamed over it. Leftovers of an interrupted write are removed, unless the process that left them is still running. Reading, a quoted value ignores what follows the closing quote, a `# comment` after a value is dropped, and a value starting with `#` is no key; a file that starts with a UTF-16 byte order mark (as Notepad and PowerShell can save it) is read as UTF-16, and written back as UTF-8. A proxy header setting (`ANTHROPIC_CUSTOM_HEADERS`) can't replace the key's header. On Windows (M13) the file is made private with an access list (§5.6), the temporary file restricted while it's still empty; a read-only target has that attribute cleared before the rename, and the rename is retried while another program holds the file. Phone access's secret and forgotten ids (§5.10) are written the same way (`src/server/owner-only.ts`), and its certificates by `certs.ts`'s own version, which doesn't block the server while it serves.
- **Windows' file locks** (M13, `src/server/fs-retry.ts`): antivirus, the search indexer and OneDrive can hold a file just written for a moment. On Windows, renames and removals in backups, card data downloads, the key file, scan images, phone access's files and the library move are retried while they fail with EPERM, EACCES or EBUSY: after 50 ms, doubling to 1 s, about 10 s in all (2 s for scan images, which a commit waits on). Elsewhere they're tried once, as before.
- **Anthropic**: typed error handling with the SDK's error classes. A refused key (401) says to check it in Settings. Rate limits, overload (529), timeouts, and server errors are retried by the SDK (twice), then shown in the chat with Continue. A `refusal` stop reason (after the fallbacks) is shown as such. A tool call cut off by `max_tokens` is never run; the answer's text is kept and marked as cut off.
- **OCR helper**: timeout or crash restarts the process, and the item goes to review with the error (§5.1.4).
- **API responses**: errors are JSON `{error: {code, message}}` with the right HTTP status (a 429 with `Retry-After`). The SPA shows toasts for mutations and inline errors for queries; on a phone, 401 `unpaired` and 403 `pc_only` are the gate's (§5.10.7). The codes phone access adds: 401 `unpaired`; 403 `pc_only`, `wrong_host`, `cross_site` and `use_https`; 413 `too_large`; 429 `rate_limited`; 400 `bad_address` and `wrong_code`; 409 `lan_off`, `not_listening`, `no_address`, `not_pairing`, `https_off`, `queue_full` and `recently_refreshed`; 500 `certificate_failed`.
- **Backups**: compact copies in the library folder's `backups/` (the project's `data/`, or the desktop app's, §3.4), made with `VACUUM INTO` (about half the live file's size), written under a temporary name and renamed when complete; a failed copy's temporary file is removed (a failure to remove it doesn't hide why the copy failed), and each backup first removes the partial copies (`binder-*.db.tmp`) that interrupted ones left. Phone access's `lan/` isn't backed up (§5.10.3). Removing old backups is best effort: one that can't be removed is logged and tried again next time. Each kind is pruned on its own, and never the backup just written. Restore one by stopping Binder and copying it over the library folder's `binder.db` (deleting `binder.db-wal` and `binder.db-shm`).
  - **Daily**: at server start, if `last_backup_at` is more than 24 h old, or dated in the future (the clock was once set ahead), write `backups/binder-YYYY-MM-DD.db` (local date) and keep the newest 7. An existing backup for today is never replaced (after a restore it may hold newer data); the start logs that it's already there. A failed backup is logged and doesn't stop the app; the next start tries again.
  - **Back up now** (Settings): writes today's backup when there is none yet, or else an extra copy, `binder-YYYY-MM-DD-N.db`, numbered after the last copy kept that day. Extra copies are kept apart from the daily 7: the newest 3, by date and then copy, so pressing it never pushes out the week of daily backups. It records `last_backup_at`, so the next daily backup waits a day from it. A failure is shown, not logged.
  - **Before upgrading**: when the server start, `pnpm run setup`, or `pnpm ocr:bench` finds a migration pending on a database that already has migrations applied, it first logs "Backing up the database before upgrading it…" and writes `backups/binder-YYYY-MM-DD-before-NNN.db`, NNN being the first pending migration (another copy that day is `…-before-NNN-2.db`, and so on, numbered after the last one kept: an earlier copy is never replaced, and a new one is never numbered below one kept, which pruning would take for older). The newest 3 of these are kept, apart from the daily 7 and the extra copies. If the copy fails, the database isn't changed: the server (or script) prints one line saying why and exits with status 1 rather than upgrade a library it couldn't back up. A new, empty database is migrated without a copy.
- **Validation**: all request bodies and query params are validated with Zod at the route boundary.

## 7. Testing
Vitest, test-first for logic modules.
- **Fixtures**: `tests/fixtures/cards.json` holds about 60 real Scryfall card objects fetched once by `scripts/fetch-fixtures.ts`. They cover DFC, split, adventure, flip, X costs, hybrid and Phyrexian mana, colorless, lands, basics, "any number" cards, partner commanders, and multiple printings of the same card.
- **Search**: a parser test table (input → AST or error), plus a compiler test table (query → expected card names over the fixture DB). Scryfall-semantics cases (`c:` vs `id:`) get explicit tests.
- **Ownership**: owned, allocated, free, short, status, and buy list across built, prospective, and maybe combinations.
- **Formats**: every rule in §4.4.
- **Decklists**: parse and serialize round-trips for each format, plus the resolution order.
- **Matcher**: synthetic OCR outputs → decisions (exact set+number, name-only, ambiguous, junk).
- **Routes**: Hono `app.request()` against an in-memory database seeded from fixtures. Covers collection CRUD, deck CRUD, buy list, library search, and scan item lifecycle (with the OCR client stubbed).
- **Sets**: the completion counts (a showcase copy counts for its card, a reprint from another set doesn't, the basic lands once each, any finish, number order) and both routes (a set with nothing owned, an unknown code), plus the page's percentages, sorts, and filter as plain functions in `src/web/lib/sets.ts`.
- **Playtest**: the game in `src/shared/playtest/`: every action; that replaying a game gives the same board as playing it; the seeded shuffle; the 10-and-7 mulligan and the bottom order; commander tax and the command-zone choice; control changes and owners' zones; tokens vanishing; default placement; the would-lose markers; and the log's wording from each seat. The routes: the deck snapshot (chosen printings, commanders in the command zone, side and maybe boards left out), saving and undo (409s, 400 for an action that doesn't apply), one game at most. In M12, the import of tokens and their links from fixture lines, and that tokens stay out of search.
- **Brainstorm**: a local fake of the Messages API (`tests/helpers/fake-anthropic.ts`) streams scripted answers in the wire format the SDK parses, so the chat loop, the tools, and the routes are tested without a key or any call to Anthropic.
- **The desktop app**: its paths, links, and messages are unit-tested, as are `startBinder` and the library move; the packaged app is checked end to end on a copy of the library with Chromium's fake camera (the M9 plan's Task 5): its window, auto mode through the OCR helper inside it, closing and opening it again, and quitting. Since M13, what differs per platform (`electron/platform.ts`) is tested for each platform on any computer: both path layouts (`path.win32` and `path.posix`), the menus, the tray's menu and tooltip, the camera's decision, `--quit`, the AppUserModelID against `package.json`, and the log's rotation when a rename or copy fails; and `pnpm app`'s Windows steps with a stand-in PC and clock (`tasklist`'s answer, asking a running Binder to quit, the installer's exit codes and its check), plus `package.json`'s build settings (uninstalling never deletes the library).
- **Portability** (M13): the suite is written to run on a Mac, a Windows PC, and Linux (as root too); its first run on Windows is one of the owner's checks. Tests run in UTC (`vitest.config.ts`); failures are injected with spies, or a folder in a file's place, never `chmod` (which root and Windows ignore); temporary folders are removed after the databases in them close (`tests/helpers/tmp.ts`); helpers' paths come through `fileURLToPath`; "private to its owner" is checked per platform (`expectOwnerOnly`: mode 600, or one icacls entry with full control and nothing inherited); timing bounds are generous (`QUICK_MS`, 1 s); and the Mac's install steps run only on a Mac. Platform logic takes the platform as an argument (paths, menus, camera ranking and errors, the OCR command, key labels, sizes), so each platform's is tested anywhere. On Linux, `VITEST_SIMULATE_WINDOWS_LOCKS=1` (`tests/helpers/windows-locks.ts`) refuses, as Windows does, to delete or rename a database file that's open, or a folder holding one, so a test that cleans up too early fails without a Windows PC.
- **OCR helpers** (M13): a fake helper (`tests/helpers/fake-ocr.ts`) answers with a byte order mark, CRLF, malformed or infinite numbers, noise, a slow start, or not at all, and refuses a request that isn't ASCII, for the client's tests. `ocr-helper.test.ts` checks each platform's command and that `ocr.ps1` is pure ASCII; with a PowerShell (`BINDER_TEST_POWERSHELL`, else Windows PowerShell on Windows, else `pwsh` on the PATH; skipped without one) it checks the script parses without what 5.1 lacks, runs `-SelfTest`, and speaks the protocol; on Windows alone, it reads a card it draws with System.Drawing, at a path with "Zoë" in it, with Windows OCR.
- **Phone access** (M13): requests as the phones' listener makes them (`makeLanApp`, `tests/helpers/app.ts`); every route's policy (a route left out of the table fails a test); the guard's Host, Origin, cookie, rate and body rules, and this computer's unchanged ones; pairing (code, key, tries per address and in all, expiry, a single phone), devices (the HMAC, forgetting one or all, a backup brought back, a library without its secret), the addresses' ranking and subnets, and phone access started and stopped with `startBinder`. The certificates are parsed back with `@peculiar/x509` (every extension and its criticality, the name constraints' exact bytes) and served over real TLS on 127.0.0.1 to a client that trusts only the authority: accepted by address and by `.local` name, refused for other addresses and by the public roots, and certificates forged with the authority's own key for names outside its constraints (a public site, a public address, an email address, a URI) refused by OpenSSL; renewal, rotation, a clock set back, and damaged files. Over HTTPS: the redirect, `use_https`, the setup page and the authority's download, pairing again, and a taken port. Windows' network profile is parsed from stand-in output.
- **The web app on a phone** (M13): the platform helpers, the clipboard's fallback, Back (a fake history: StrictMode, stacked overlays, replaced entries), CSV bytes, the gate's decisions, the pairing helpers, the QR code's path, the photo's crop and guide, the playtest's presses, and Settings on a phone and on the PC (its sections, and the queries they ask) rendered to markup.
- **Manual**: the scanner end-to-end with the iPhone (in Binder.app too), brainstorm with a real key, and a whole Commander playtest in Binder.app. The playtest board is also checked headlessly (setup, mulligans, a few turns, dragging, tapping, selecting, undo, switching side, and resuming after a reload) on a copy of the library. M13's pages at phone width, by emulated touch, the pairing flow and Settings → Phone access were checked headlessly in Chromium; Windows itself, a real Android phone, and the Mac's parts M13 changed are the owner's first-use checks (`docs/plans/m13-followups.md`).
